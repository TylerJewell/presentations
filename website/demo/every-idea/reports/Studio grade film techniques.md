# Test motion blur, grain and encoding first

The sources point to three changes for the one-minute and five-minute films: sub-frame motion blur, a deterministic grain layer applied before the final encode, and a 10-bit intermediate. The support for the first two comes from vendor documentation and practitioner notes, and no source measured the perceived effect of either. The 10-bit change conflicts with the 8-bit H.264 specification in the brief, and the notes do not show that the target platforms accept High 10 files. The voice is the second area of risk. Vendor sources attribute flat synthetic narration to delivery and script, and the current method of one sentence per clip with fixed pauses is the element they most directly question. No source gives a words-per-minute, shot-length or budget figure for a film of this kind, so the length targets rest on secondary snippets. Current generative tools produce usable backplates and atmosphere, and no source reviewed found acceptable generated on-screen text, interface or logos.

The same post states that the film was built in Blender, with EEVEE and Cycles renders, and contains no live action.

Product interfaces appear in the reference films as abstractions, not as screen recordings. Notion's campaign states that its team **created a consistent set of abstracted UI ingredients to punctuate each story** ([Instrument, Notion](https://www.instrument.com/work/notion); the notes record this from search-result text, not a full fetch). Figma's campaign shows no interface on screen. Its graphics respond to close-up shots of a hand clicking ([ITSNICETHAT, 15 January 2026](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/figma-make-prompt-it-then-push-it-campaign-advertising-150126); fetched). The writer's description was not checked against the film. Tendril's IBM Think 2026 films reinterpret UI elements as **glass and subsurface materials** ([Shots.net](https://shots.net/news/view/ibm-launches-new-campaign-from-tendril); search-result summary, unverified in full).

Practical staging appears in one live-action reference. OpenAI's Time to Fly film used large sets built on custom gimbals, so the environments could tilt around the performers ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/openai-codex-time-to-fly-alto-smuggler); search snippet). One source dates the film to 3 June 2026, and another dates it to 14 July 2026. The Nothing Playground film by BUCK credits Zelig Sound for sound design. Its page describes the visual language as **grids, pixels, dots, and transparency** ([BUCK, Nothing Playground](https://buck.co/work/nothing-playground); fetched 18 November 2026, date not stated on the page). Anthropic's Hope in Hard Questions **opens on footage of a burning house at night**, followed by a voice asking the opening question ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/anthropic-confronts-the-hard-questions-about-ai-responsibility/); fetched). The film's runtime is not confirmed.

The Motion Awards 2025 winners page names three winners, and none of them is a brand, product or explainer entry ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2025/12/08/the-motion-awards-2025-winners/); fetched). No 2026 results were found.

## Motion blur and easing carry the strongest code-side support

Remotion documents `HtmlInCanvasMotionBlur` as its recommended motion blur. The component blends sub-frame samples for each output frame ([Remotion motion blur guide](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/motion-blur-guide); cited finding, the notes do not record a fetch). The notes give the exposure formula as (shutter angle / 360) / fps. At 30 fps and a 180-degree shutter, each frame exposes for 16.7 ms, which is arithmetic from that formula. The same guide states that `CameraMotionBlur` can distort colour and opacity, and that `Trail` repeats earlier positions rather than blending frames.

A custom harness that averages N captures per output frame multiplies the capture count by N. The one-minute film has **1,800 frames** and the five-minute film has **9,000 frames** at 30 fps. Eight samples per frame produce **14,400 captures** for the first and **72,000 captures** for the second. The notes found no source giving render time per sample at 1920x1080, and no source on the colour cost of averaging 8-bit frames.

Easing is the cheapest of the named moves to implement. Emil Kowalski, a product UI engineer, recommends ease-out curves for entering elements and a duration under 300 ms for interface motion ([Emil Kowalski, 7 practical animation tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips); fetched, undated). That guidance covers interface interaction, not film. A search snippet attributes to a course the claim that **a small amount of easing immediately improves the perceived quality of the motion** ([CreativeLive](https://www.creativelive.com/class/adobe-after-effects-fundamentals-philip-ebiner-fiverr/lessons/13-tips-to-make-your-animations-look-more-nautral-04-22); snippet, unverified as a quote). An ease curve applied to position as a function of t fits the pipeline without an outside tool.

The pmndrs `postprocessing` library lists a NoiseEffect for film grain and a DepthOfFieldEffect for bokeh ([postprocessing 6.39.4 README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/postprocessing@6.39.4/README.md); cited finding). The notes found no source for halation or chromatic aberration as named effects. That library needs a Three.js scene, so using it changes the rendering stack.

WebGPU raises a capture risk for this pipeline. Chrome and Edge support WebGPU on Windows with Direct3D 12 from version 113 ([web.dev, WebGPU support](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers); cited finding). One third-party guide reports that headless WebGPU captures come out black on Windows, and recommends a headed browser in an interactive session ([agent-browser, WebGPU guide](https://agent-browser.dev/webgpu); third-party, unverified). The pipeline runs Playwright headless. Any WebGPU scene therefore needs a black-frame check on its first frame before a full render. Headless Chromium uses SwiftShader software rendering unless GPU flags are set ([Chromium GPU docs](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src.git/+show/main/docs/gpu/server-side-headless-linux-chrome-with-gpus.md); Linux-focused).

Screen Studio names smooth cursor, click effects and automatic zoom as features, and runs on macOS only ([Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/); fetched, undated). Rotato names 3D reflections and shadow styles, and runs on macOS and a web beta with no Windows version ([Rotato](https://www.rotato.app/); fetched, undated). These feature names confirm that the effects exist in those products. The notes found no page that explains how either product computes shadows or reflections.

## Encoding and grain decide whether dark gradients band

YouTube's recommended SDR encoding for 1080p at 24, 25 or 30 frames per second is **8 Mbps**. The page specifies H.264 High Profile at 4:2:0 with CABAC, and its HDR figure for 1080p is 10 Mbps ([YouTube, recommended upload encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en); platform fact, fetched 18 November 2026). The same page recommends AAC-LC audio at 48 kHz and 384 kbps stereo.

The same page states that wide-gamut content needing high bit depth converts to 8-bit BT.709 when no HDR transfer function is present, and that full-range content converts to limited range ([YouTube, same page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en); platform fact from the fetch summary, exact wording to verify on the live page). Both conversions act on near-black gradients. The notes did not measure how YouTube treats dark gradients, so this stays a risk to test on the uploaded file.

Dither inside a lossy encoder reduces banding. A dither applied before a lossy encode is removed by that encode, so the dither belongs close to the final bitstream ([Forasoft, dithering glossary](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/video-encoding/glossary/terms/dithering); practitioner, vendor glossary). One VideoHelp user reports that film grain acted as natural dither ([VideoHelp forum](https://forum.videohelp.com/showthread.php?p=2475674); user forum, anecdotal).

The notes recommend a 10-bit intermediate and a libx264 High 10 encode with yuv420p10le. That recommendation departs from the 8-bit H.264 specification in the brief. Two facts block the change until the producer decides. The YouTube page lists H.264 High Profile for SDR, not High 10. The notes did not check 10-bit H.264 playback on the target platforms. The notes treat a 10-bit master as the safe source for every platform, and that is an inference that needs a test on each platform's uploaded file.

The brief sets the music bed at -16 LUFS. AES TD1008, dated September 2021, gives **-18 LUFS** for speech and **-16 LUFS** for music, with a **-1 dBTP** ceiling, according to two secondary sources ([Fora Soft, LUFS targets per platform 2026](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lufs-targets-per-platform-2026); [Telos Alliance, loudness for streaming](https://docs.telosalliance.com/docs/understanding-loudness-for-streaming-audio); secondary). The AES PDF returned HTTP 403, so the primary was not read. A narration-led film matches the speech figure more closely than the music figure. The choice belongs to the producer. A vendor page claims YouTube normalises to about -14 LUFS, which is unverified.

Film mixes divide dialogue, music and effects into stems labelled DX, MX and FX. International delivery requires an undipped music stem and an M&E stem ([ScreenSkills, sound mixing module](https://www.screenskills.com/media/to0jlymy/screenskills-sound-mixing-word-format.docx); [Robin Hoffmann, mix delivery in stems](https://www.robin-hoffmann.com/?p=3971); practitioner sources, unverified in full). The pipeline produces voice, music and effects as separate files, so it can keep these stems without a change to the render.

Grain guidance is thin and mostly from vendors. Frame.io's 2023 colourist piece covers when to use grain ([Frame.io, How a Pro Colorist Uses Film Grain](https://blog.frame.io/2023/08/14/how-a-pro-colorist-uses-film-grain/); older, the article's advice was not read). A production guide advises grain low enough to read as film rather than as an effect ([Dehancer, order-of-operations guide](https://cdn-files.dehancer.com/01531b9d-c43e-4c08-9ca7-1d3250c53b09_Dehancer+Ae+Pr+Plugin+Quick+Guide.pdf); vendor). Search snippets advise keeping grain off text and at low opacity; these are unverified.

## Narration and generated footage carry the least verified guidance

ElevenLabs released Eleven v4 on 28 September 2026. The vendor page states that Instant Voice Clones **capture voices with high fidelity using just 10 seconds of audio**. The page also states that v4 accepts natural-language delivery direction and inline tags, and that it improves IPA pronunciation support ([ElevenLabs, Eleven v4](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/eleven-v4); vendor claims, verified on the vendor page). The page does not state pricing. A secondary source reports a temporary API price of $22 per million characters for v4, which is unverified.

A secondary guide for v3 states that its audio tags insert an audio event at the tag's position, and that v3 ignores break and phoneme tags ([Runware, directing with audio tags](https://runware.ai/docs/models/elevenlabs-v3/guides/directing-with-audio-tags); secondary). A CDN-hosted copy of ElevenLabs prompting guidance states that **stability is the most important setting**, and that Creative mode is more prone to hallucination ([ElevenLabs prompting guide, CDN test-host copy](https://cdntest.elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting); secondary copy).

No primary source gives a words-per-minute standard for narrated brand films. Search snippets give a documentary range of 120 to 150 words per minute, and one snippet states that speech above 160 words per minute reads as an explainer ([Typecast, words per minute](https://typecast.ai/learn/?p=15068); snippet, unverified). Every source that gave a range said that pauses change total length, and recommended a test read timed against the audio.

Viewing data points in one direction. Vidyard's 2025 benchmark states that **65% of viewers of videos under one minute stay engaged to the end**, against **20% of viewers of videos over 20 minutes** ([Vidyard, Video in Business Benchmark](https://www.vidyard.com/video-benchmark/); fetched; the data year is unverified). The same page states that the highest engagement falls within the first quarter of a video, without a percentage. Wistia's 2026 report states that **the shorter the video, the higher the engagement rate** ([Wistia, 2026 State of Video](https://www.wistia.com/state-of-video); fetched, published 22 April 2026). Both data sets cover B2B video. Neither covers a brand film on a company's own site.

Vendor sources attribute flat synthetic narration to **fixed pitch, even timing and weak stress on key words**, and they attribute the flatness to delivery rather than to the voice model ([Creatify, how to add AI voice to video](https://creatify.ai/blog/how-to-add-ai-voice-to-video); vendor, snippet, unverified). One vendor recommends sentences of 8 to 15 words and reading each line aloud before generation ([AmmarAI](https://ammarai-creative-hub.lovable.app/blog/how-to-generate-ai-voiceovers); vendor, snippet, unverified). A practitioner states that stripping breaths and pitch drift can make a voice sound fake ([Respeecher, how to make AI voice sound human](https://www.respeecher.com/blog/how-to-make-ai-voice-sound-human); snippet dated 14 April 2026, full text not read).

The brief's current method sets one sentence per clip with fixed pauses. The notes found no source that names uniform pauses as a tell. The notes infer that fixed pauses produce a list-like rhythm, and that pauses tied to each picture change, plus at least one passage with breath-length gaps, would correct it. Room tone is the other element the sources name. Vendor sources state that a light room-tone bed or background sound masks the over-clean quality of synthetic speech ([Creatify](https://creatify.ai/blog/how-to-add-ai-voice-to-video); vendor, unverified). The brief specifies no room tone.

The notes did not check consent, rights or platform policy for voice clones. EU AI Act Article 50 requires providers to mark synthetic output in a machine-readable form, and requires deployers to disclose deepfakes ([artificialintelligenceact.eu, Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); primary text, verified in the notes). Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. Whether a cloned narrator counts as a deepfake under the Act is not established, and the notes call for legal review.

Google Veo 3.1 became generally available on 17 November 2025. Its Generate tier outputs up to 4K, and its Fast and Lite tiers output up to 1080p. Clips run 4, 6 or 8 seconds. The documented output frame rate is **24 FPS**, which differs from the pipeline's 30 fps, so each clip needs frame-rate conversion. The page lists C2PA Content Credentials and states no indemnity ([Google Cloud, Veo 3.1 documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/veo/3-1-generate); primary, verified in the notes). OpenAI discontinued Sora as a consumer product. Sources conflict on the shutdown dates, and one vendor blog schedules removal of the Sora 2 API aliases for 24 September 2026 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-is-shutting-down-sora-its-powerful-ai-video-app); secondary; [invideo, Sora](https://invideo.io/blog/sora-ai-video-generator/); vendor blog).

Adobe states that Firefly Video trains only on content it has permission to use. That is a vendor claim. A June 2026 review rated the claim substantiated, and states that it is not a legal audit ([Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/adobe-launches-commercially-safe-ai-video-generation-model/); vendor; [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/adobe-firefly-ai-video-generator-review); secondary).

Public cases show backlash to generated people and stitched clips. McDonald's Netherlands pulled a generated Christmas spot after criticism of its uncanny visuals ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/mcdonalds-pulls-ai-generated-christmas-ad-after-backlash-over-soulless-visuals-and-holiday-chaos); cited in the notes, fetch not recorded). One secondary source reports that Cannes Lions revoked a Grand Prix after a case-study film presented AI footage as real events ([Pulse Advertising](https://www.pulse-advertising.com/?p=20820); secondary; verify against trade press). The notes found no backlash to abstract, non-figurative material. That absence is an inference, not a finding.

For the music bed, Warner Music Group settled with Suno in November 2025 and agreed to co-develop licensed models ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-group-settles-with-suno/); secondary). Suno's paid-tier download limits also apply to any bed made there ([DubSpot](https://blog.dubspot.com/suno-udio-ai-music-lawsuits-2026); secondary). The notes recommend checking the terms of the plan used before the bed ships.

## What the research did not verify

The notes contain no viewing of any film. Every reference-film entry rests on studio text, press text, credit listings or search snippets. The notes give no shot length, words-per-minute figure or cut count for any named benchmark film. The reference set covers eight companies. The notes found no verified 2025 or 2026 film for Linear, Stripe or Cursor.

No source measured the perceived quality of motion blur, grain, grade, easing or sound. The ranking of techniques in this report is a synthesis of practitioner statements. It is not a measured finding.

Several source gaps affect the recommendations directly. The AES TD1008 PDF returned HTTP 403, so the loudness figures rest on two secondary sources. The YouTube page's wording on near-black conversion was not checked on the live page. The Vimeo and LinkedIn bitrate tables were not confirmed from their own pages, and the notes do not cite their third-party figures as platform values. No test of headless WebGPU capture in Chromium or Edge was run.

Other areas were not covered. The notes contain no source on wide-gamut or HDR capture through Chromium, and none on ProRes or H.265 10-bit with ffmpeg. No source names halation or chromatic aberration as a named effect with a method. No budget or timeline source exists for sound design on a film of this length. Studios beyond Zelig Sound were not searched for sound work.

Several dates conflict and remain unresolved. The Three.js native Gaussian splat release date differs between two sources. The Remotion 4.0.4xx release dates were not confirmed. Veo 4 has no official source. Two dates for the Anthropic film conflict, September 2025 and mid-2026. The Ramp campaign year conflicts, between 2025 and 2026. One source reports that Apple's September 2026 keynote film echoes Skyfall, from low-reliability sites, and this report does not use it.

## Conclusion

The sources do not rank the techniques by effect. Easing, motion blur and grain are each cheap to test in this pipeline, because each is a function of t or a per-frame layer. The useful next step is a controlled test. Render one 15-second scene in three variants: no blur or grain, blur only, and blur with grain. Upload each variant to a private or unlisted test on the target platform, and compare the dark gradients in the processed file. That test answers the question the sources do not answer, and each variant costs one render.

The encoding decision carries the largest unresolved conflict. The brief's 8-bit H.264 specification and the notes' 10-bit recommendation cannot both hold until the platform check is complete. The producer should decide after the test, using the processed files. The narration change is a script and timing change first. It requires no new tool. The voice-consent review is a separate step, and the notes did not cover it.
