## 1. Operating models: centralised, federated, hub-and-spoke, a global baseline with local overlays, and when each works

### Takeaway
Firms that publish their set-up describe a hybrid. A central committee sets principles, the risk taxonomy and an inventory. Business-line or country "councils" or "focal points" run the reviews. Controls are tiered by materiality rather than set to a strictest common denominator. Regulators now write this structure in explicitly: MAS lets a Singapore entity "leverage" its group's AI framework if that framework meets local expectations. The failure mode that published sources document is uneven implementation across decentralised units, not conflict between laws.

### Cited Findings
- **McKinsey (March 2025 report; survey 16–31 July 2024, 1,491 respondents).** The report says "Risk and data governance are two of the most centralized elements of deploying AI solutions, whereas tech talent is often hybrid", and that for these functions organisations "often use a fully centralized model such as a center of excellence." The exhibit figures are risk and compliance 57 / 30 / 13 and data governance for AI 46 / 39 / 15 across fully centralised, hybrid and fully decentralised. The extracted legend is jumbled: one summary reads 13% as the centralised share, while the narrative implies 57% and 46% are centralised. Check against the PDF before use. [Search-summary] — [McKinsey summary via ABN Asia PDF mirror](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/abncharts/abncharts.public.1/abnasia.org/1744132962976_www.abnasia.org.pdf); [briefing summary](https://artificialintellitools.blogspot.com/2025/04/briefing-document-state-of-ai-how.html)
- **HSBC (hub and councils).** "During 1H25, building on our Group AI Review Committee, we embedded AI Review Councils across the organisation and refined AI lifecycle management." HSBC also runs group-wide mandatory responsible-AI training through an AI Academy. [Search-summary of HSBC's own page] — [HSBC, Transforming HSBC with AI](https://www.hsbc.com/who-we-are/hsbc-and-digital/hsbc-and-ai/transforming-hsbc-with-ai). The 2026 interim report says HSBC continues "to enhance our Group-wide AI oversight, governance, lifecycle management and risk framework", including for AI facilitated through third parties. [Search-summary] — [HSBC 6-K 1H2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001089113/000108911326000025/hsbc-20260630.htm). A first Group Chief AI Officer (David Rice) was reported, effective 1 April 2026. [Search-summary, single trade source] — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/international/global-payments/2025/inside-hsbcs-push-to-marry-ai-with-accountability)
- **Standard Chartered (federated, in 41 markets).** A Responsible AI Council chaired by the Group Chief Data Officer reviews models against the bank's principles before approval and "stands up as and when needed" rather than meeting monthly. A separate AI Safety Council oversees the bank-wide AI inventory. Bank materials describe business units having flexibility within enterprise-wide controls. SC GPT went live across 41 markets in March 2025 for over 70,000 staff. [Search-summary, trade press] — [The Digital Banker](https://thedigitalbanker.com/making-ai-adoption-safe-how-standard-chartered-is-putting-humans-in-control-of-ai/); [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/standard-chartered-s-risk-first-approach-to-scaling-ai-globally)
- **IBM (hub and spoke with focal points).** The AI Ethics Board is a "cross-functional and centralized" body. AI Ethics Focal Points in each business unit are "points of first contact" who triage low-risk cases. Higher-risk cases get a formal risk assessment and go up to the Board. A CPO AI Ethics Project Office sets Board agendas, and a grassroots Advocacy Network supports the structure. [Search-summary; IBM's own materials] — [IBM, A look into IBM's AI ethics governance framework](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/a-look-into-ibms-ai-ethics-governance-framework)
- **AstraZeneca (decentralised under shared principles; 2021 audit, published 2022, so older).** AstraZeneca is "a decentralised organisation in which different business areas operate independently". Each business area could build its own governance structure "as long as these align with the externally published ethics principles". Four enterprise-wide mechanisms support this: a compliance document, a Responsible AI Playbook, an AI resolution board with a Responsible AI Consultancy Service, and the AI audit. Business areas "understood risk differently". The authors recommend harmonising compliance assurance across areas while adapting risk assurance locally. "It took AstraZeneca over six months to formulate the principles and another year to embed them across the business", and some units were still unaware of the compliance document during the audit. [Primary] — [Mökander & Floridi, AI and Ethics 3(2) 2023, online 31 May 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9152664/)
- **Unilever (central assurance service).** Every new AI application is proposed before development and reviewed internally, and complex cases go to external experts. External partner Holistic AI has been involved since 2021. Use cases get red, yellow or green ratings, and "only a handful of the several hundred" use cases have received red. Unilever coverage cites a milestone of 150 "projects assured" and over 500 AI systems in operation. Holistic AI's own case study claims 300+ projects governed and a 50% risk reduction, which is vendor marketing. [Search-summary] — [MIT Sloan Management Review, "AI Ethics at Unilever: From Policy to Process"](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/ai-ethics-at-unilever-from-policy-to-process); [Unilever on EU AI Act preparation (2024)](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/the-eu-ai-act-has-arrived-how-unilever-is-preparing); [Holistic AI case study (vendor)](https://www.holisticai.com/how-we-helped-unilever)
- **Tiering, not strictest common denominator (Singapore banks).** In MAS's mid-2024 thematic review, most banks applied baseline standards to all AI and enhanced standards to higher-materiality uses. Most required independent validation only for higher-materiality AI and peer review for the rest. Several banks set up cross-functional AI oversight forums on top of existing risk governance. [Search-summary of law-firm write-ups; the MAS page itself only confirms the review's scope and date (5 Dec 2024)] — [MAS information paper page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/monographs-or-information-paper/2024/artificial-intelligence-model-risk-management); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/05/good-practices-for-ai-model-risk-management-for-singapore-financ.html); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/29565/mas-publishes-information-paper-on-ai-model-risk-management)
- **A regulator endorses group frameworks (MAS final Guidelines, 7 Oct 2026).** A Singapore FI in a global group "may take guidance from or leverage their group's AI risk management frameworks", provided they meet the Guidelines' expectations. The Guidelines apply "commensurate with the size and nature of an FI's activities". They take effect 7 Oct 2027, with sections 5–6 to be met by 7 Oct 2028. [Secondary, opened] — [Allen & Gledhill, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)
- **Split accountability is the norm (UK FS, survey 2024, published Nov 2024, so older).** 84% of firms reported having an accountable person for their AI framework, and 72% said executive leadership was accountable for AI use cases. Most firms reported "three or more accountable persons or bodies". International banks (18 respondents) answered for UK operations only. [Primary] — [Bank of England/FCA, AI in UK financial services 2024](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024)
- **Policy-level two-layer model.** The WEF proposes a shared "constitutional core" of standards and accountability rules, with a "local overlay" where each jurisdiction applies context-specific rules. This describes governments, not a firm's operating model. [Search-summary] — [WEF, Nov 2025](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/11/trust-ai-global-governance/)

### Inferences
- The model that can be shown on a slide is a central hub that owns principles, taxonomy, inventory and the escalation threshold, plus spokes (HSBC councils, IBM focal points, AstraZeneca business areas) that triage and run reviews. Materiality tiering, rather than a "strictest everywhere" rule, decides what goes up to the hub (MAS banks, IBM triage).
- Published sources trace failures to central principles that reach units unevenly (AstraZeneca: units unaware, risk understood differently, 18 months to embed) and to accountability spread across three or more bodies (BoE). They do not document failures caused by conflicting national rules.
- MAS's "leverage the group framework" clause and EU AI Act Art. 17(4)/26(5) (see section 7) are the regulator-side counterpart to a global baseline with local overlays: a group framework is accepted if it meets the local floor.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study comparing operating models across multinationals and their outcomes, beyond single-firm cases (AstraZeneca). Searches turned up policy-level papers (WEF, UN) only.
- I found no published firm example of an explicit "strictest common denominator" policy for AI. It appears in practitioner FAQs, not in sourced company disclosures.
- Novartis, Roche, Siemens, JPMorgan Chase, ING and Salesforce were not researched within the call budget. Their country-variation practices are unverified.

## 2. Mapping one control set to many regimes: unified control frameworks, crosswalks, ISO/IEC 42001, harmonised standards, OECD HAIP reporting

### Takeaway
There is no official multi-regime crosswalk that firms can adopt wholesale. NIST lists a Microsoft-contributed crosswalk from the AI RMF to ISO/IEC 42001. Academic unified control frameworks propose about 42 controls mapped to regulations. ISO/IEC 42001 is not a harmonised standard under the EU AI Act. The first AI Act harmonised standard (EN 18286, quality management) was approved in July 2026, and whether it has been cited in the Official Journal is unconfirmed. ISO/IEC 42001 certification is still rare (hundreds of organisations) and concentrated among US software vendors, not the banks, pharma and consumer-goods firms this talk is about.

### Cited Findings
- **The Unified Control Framework (Credo AI authors, arXiv March 2025)** combines a risk taxonomy, policy requirements drawn from regulations, and "a parsimonious set of 42 controls that simultaneously address multiple risk scenarios and compliance requirements". It was validated by mapping it to the Colorado AI Act. [Search-summary] — [Eisenberg, Gamboa, Sherman, arXiv:2503.05937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05937v1)
- **NIST crosswalk.** NIST's AI Resource Center reportedly lists a crosswalk contributed by Microsoft that maps AI RMF functions and categories to ISO/IEC 42001 clauses and Annex A controls. I did not find or open the NIST URL. A separate 16-page third-party mapping (Sept 2026) "makes no claim of equivalence". [Search-summary] — [CASRAI comparison](https://casrai.org/compare/nist-ai-rmf-vs-iso-42001); [AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/nist-ai-rmf-to-iso-iec-fdis-42001-ai-management-system-crosswalk/)
- **ISO/IEC 42001 adoption, official count.** ISO Survey 2025: 271 valid ISO/IEC 42001 certificates (covering 572 sites, in 40 countries) as of 31 Dec 2025. This is the first edition that includes 42001. [Search-summary; the reproduction I saw was an AI-assisted DQS article, and ISO's data is behind IAF CertSearch] — [DQS, ISO Survey 2025](https://www.dqsglobal.com/es/acerca-de/sala-de-prensa/iso-survey-2025-las-certificaciones-iso-crecen-un-11-en-el-mundo-y-un-28-en-espana); [ISO Survey](https://www.iso.org/the-iso-survey.html)
- **ISO/IEC 42001 adoption, public announcements (data to 25 Oct 2026).** 229 publicly announced certified organisations: 19 in 2024, 89 in 2025, 120 in 2026 to date. They span 30 countries with 28 certifying bodies. By headquarters: US 148 (65%), India 12, UK 9. By industry: enterprise software 44%, professional services 15%, cloud and AI platforms 8%, cybersecurity 7%. The directory is not verified against accreditation registries and is English-biased. [Primary for the directory; the directory is unofficial] — [ISO42k, State of ISO 42001](https://www.iso42k.com/state-of-iso-42001). Another estimate based on announcements is about 350 organisations by spring 2026. [Search-summary] — [Atoro](https://atoro.io/how-many-companies-are-iso-42001-certified/)
- **Harmonised standards.** EN 18286:2026 (quality management system for AI Act Art. 17) was reportedly approved by CEN-CENELEC on 12 July 2026, the first JTC 21 AI Act standard to reach final approval. Intertek (29 Sept 2026) said it was not yet cited in the OJEU. One source says Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2274 cited it on 14 Oct 2026; that source is single and unverified. The essential requirements of Chapter III Section 2 are covered by other JTC 21 deliverables, still mostly in development. JTC 21 says ISO/IEC 42001 "does not cover the full set of QMS requirements of the regulation". [Search-summary] — [Intertek, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.intertek.com/blog/2026/10-01-en-18286-first-eu-ai-act-standards-puzzle-piece-is-on-the-table/); [CSA research note, 25 Oct 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-en18286-harmonised-standard-presumption-20/); [Modulos](https://docs.modulos.ai/frameworks/eu-ai-act/harmonized-standards/en-18286); [TÜV](https://consulting.tuv.com/aktuelles/ki-im-fokus/harmonisierte-normen-ai-act)
- **OECD reporting framework for the G7 Hiroshima code (HAIP).** Launched 7 Feb 2025, with first reports due 15 April 2025. The first round produced 25 reports, which the OECD analysed in "How are AI developers managing risks?". Reports are published on OECD.AI and can be updated annually, and an organisation can be delisted if its conduct departs from its report. A "2.0" pilot reportedly broadens participation to deployers and SMEs (June 2026). [Search-summary] — [OECD.AI HAIP overview](https://oecd.ai/fr/transparency/overview); [Covington Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2025/02/oecd-launches-voluntary-reporting-framework-on-ai-risk-management-practices/); [OECD.AI FAQ](https://oecd.ai/en/transparency/faq)
- **Microsoft's internal mechanism.** An internal workflow tool built to "centralize the various responsible AI requirements outlined in the Responsible AI Standard" and support documentation for pre-deployment reviews. [Primary] — [Microsoft, 2025 Responsible AI Transparency Report blog, 20 June 2025](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2025/06/20/our-2025-responsible-ai-transparency-report/)

### Inferences
- In practice, "map once, comply many" means an internal control library (Microsoft's Standard and tool, the UCF's 42 controls) with crosswalk columns per regime. No regulator has approved such a mapping.
- ISO/IEC 42001 does not yet serve as a passport for regulated multinationals. About two-thirds of announced certificates are US companies and about 44% are enterprise software. The pattern looks like vendors certifying to sell to regulated buyers. Banks and pharma appear to rely on sector supervision instead.
- The UCF's validation target, the Colorado AI Act, was repealed and replaced in May 2026 (trends.md §2.5). This is a live example of a crosswalk going stale because the regime it maps to changed.

### Gaps
- I did not open the NIST crosswalk page. Its version, date and whether NIST publishes an AI RMF to EU AI Act crosswalk are unverified.
- I found no figures on how many multinationals in banking, insurance, pharma or consumer goods hold ISO/IEC 42001.
- The list of HAIP reporters after round one and the current count were not found. I could not confirm which of the named companies (Microsoft, IBM, Salesforce and others) filed.
- The OJ citation of EN 18286 needs checking on EUR-Lex.

## 3. Direct conflicts: data localisation versus central monitoring, local accountable persons, works councils, different log-retention periods, different review rights

### Takeaway
The conflicts are specific and concrete. China, India, Vietnam and Saudi Arabia impose a transfer mechanism or a local copy on logs that contain personal data. Korea and the EU require a named local representative. French courts have suspended AI roll-outs until the works council was consulted, and German co-determination applies where AI can monitor employees. Minimum retention periods differ: EU deployer logs at least 6 months, India CERT-In 180 days kept in India, India DPDP Rules reportedly 1 year, EU authorised-representative documents 10 years. Published resolutions are mechanical: process in-country and export only what is permitted, use intra-group certification, appoint a local representative, sign group-level framework agreements, and keep a retention schedule set to the longest period that applies.

### Cited Findings
**Data localisation and cross-border transfer**
- **China.** The CAC's April 2025 FAQ says the regime "mainly regulates important data and personal information, and other types of data may be freely transferred overseas". The October 2025 FAQ says exemptions are to be "narrowly construed", and that overseas personnel who access data locally in mainland China, without moving it abroad, do not trigger a transfer. The CAC and SAMR issued Measures for Certification of Cross-Border Personal Information Transfer on 14 Oct 2025, and the certification specification covers transfers within multinational groups. [Search-summary] — [Arnold & Porter, June 2025](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/06/china-clarifies-cross-border-data-transfer-rules); [Arnold & Porter, Nov 2025](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/11/china-issues-clarifications-cross-border-data-transfer-rules); [China Briefing, Oct 2025](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/chinas-cross-border-data-transfer-qa-iii-october-2025/)
- **China, filing as a local-registration gate.** Cumulative CAC generative-AI service filings: 796 as of 28 Feb 2026 and 988 as of 30 June 2026. 124 more were added in July–August 2026, for 1,112 (notice 14 Sept 2026). Apps that call a filed model through an API go through registration with local or provincial cyberspace authorities ("double filing"). Live products must display the model name and filing number. [Search-summary; aggregators, not the CAC text] — [Digital Policy Alert, Jan–Feb 2026](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38517-cyberspace-administration-of-china-announced-registered-generative-ai-service-information-for-january-to-february-2026); [Digital Policy Alert, Jul–Aug 2026](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/43685-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-july-to-august-2026); [AppInChina (vendor)](https://appinchina.co/blog/the-complete-guide-to-chinas-aigc-registration/)
- **India.** CERT-In Directions (April 2022, so older): covered entities must "maintain [ICT logs] securely for a rolling period of 180 days ... within Indian jurisdiction" and provide them to CERT-In on request. CERT-In's FAQs reportedly allow storage abroad if a copy is kept in India, although some compliance checklists read it more strictly. The DPDP Rules 2025 reportedly require data fiduciaries and processors to keep logs for at least one year (one law-firm source). [Search-summary] — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/obligations-under-cert-in-and-dpdp-not-a-zero-sum-game); [Khaitan & Co (2022)](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2022-04/Ergo_30042022.pdf); [KS&K on DPDP retention](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/data-retention-and-deletion-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)
- **Vietnam.** Law 91/2025/QH15 on personal data protection has been in force since 1 Jan 2026, with implementing regulations adopted 31 Dec 2025. A "transfer" now includes processing locally collected data on foreign platforms. Each transfer needs a transfer impact assessment, filed with the Ministry of Public Security within 60 days, and TIA and DPIA records must be updated every six months or within 10 days of significant changes. Fines reach 5% of prior-year revenue. There is an exemption for employers storing employee data in the cloud. [Search-summary] — [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/vietnams-pdpl-in-focus-what-to-know-and-watch-for/); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/36569-implementing-regulations-of-law-on-personal-data-protection-including-cross-border-data-transfer-regulation-entered-into-force); [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/pwc-vietnam-new-personal-data-protection-law)
- **Saudi Arabia.** SDAIA updated the Regulation on Personal Data Transfer outside the Kingdom on 1 Sept 2024 and issued a four-phase Risk Assessment Guideline in Feb 2025. Sources disagree on what triggers a risk assessment: reliance on safeguards, or continuous or widespread transfer of sensitive data. [Search-summary] — [Clyde & Co, Mar 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines); [Mayer Brown, Oct 2024](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/updates-to-saudi-arabias-personal-data-protection-regulations-sccs-guidelines-and-more)
- **UK financial firms rank data protection first.** It was the largest perceived regulatory constraint on AI use. [Primary, 2024] — [BoE/FCA 2024](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024)

**A local accountable person or representative**
- **Korea (AI Basic Act, in force 22 Jan 2026; see trends.md §4.2).** Under the Enforcement Decree, a foreign operator with no Korean address must appoint a domestic representative if it meets any of three thresholds: prior-year total revenue of KRW 1 trillion (about US$662–750m depending on source), AI-service revenue of KRW 10 billion, or 1 million average daily Korean users. The representative is MSIT's contact for submissions and inspections. Fines reach KRW 30 million, after at least a one-year grace period. [Search-summary; sources differ on whether the user metric is a 3-month average] — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/south-korea-s-ai-basic-act); [US ITA](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act); [Mondaq/Kim & Chang-style overview of the Decree](https://www.mondaq.com/new-technology/1682200/a-closer-look-at-koreas-foundational-ai-act-the-presidential-decree-clarifies-a-path-for-regulation-and-innovation)
- **EU AI Act Art. 22.** Third-country providers of high-risk systems "shall, by written mandate, appoint an authorised representative". The representative must keep the declaration of conformity and the documentation "for a period of 10 years", give authorities information on reasoned request, and "terminate the mandate" and "immediately inform the relevant market surveillance authority" if the provider acts contrary to its obligations. [Primary text via mirror] — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art. 22](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/22/)

**Works councils and co-determination**
- **France.** In a summary order of 14 Feb 2025 (RG 24/01457), the Nanterre judicial court suspended the roll-out of AI applications until the CSE (works council) consultation was complete. It held that even the pilot phase required consultation, with a penalty reported at €1,000 per day for 90 days. Créteil suspended AI tools at a press group on 15 July 2025, and Nanterre ruled again on 29 Jan 2026 (CS Group France). On 10 Feb 2026, however, a Paris order held that Copilot 365 was not an "important project" requiring consultation. [Search-summary] — [CMS, "L'IA suspendue"](https://cms.law/fr/fra/legal-updates/l-ia-suspendue-le-juge-exige-la-consultation-du-cse-avant-tout-deploiement); [CMS, "quand les effets comptent plus que la technologie"](https://cms.law/fr/fra/legal-updates/deploiement-de-l-ia-quand-les-effets-comptent-plus-que-la-technologie); [FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/ia-en-entreprise-la-jurisprudence-qui-change-la-donne-et-pas-dans-une-moindre-mesure/454213)
- **Germany.** Under §87(1) No. 6 BetrVG the works council co-determines technical equipment able to monitor employee behaviour or performance, and a conciliation committee decides on deadlock. §90 names AI explicitly but gives only information and consultation rights. §80(3) lets the council call in an AI expert at the employer's cost. The Hamburg Labour Court (2024, older) found no co-determination right where employees used ChatGPT via a browser on their own accounts. Where a system spans several group companies, the group works council is competent. [Search-summary] — [Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/germany-does-works-council-have-say-when-employees-use-chatgpt-no-according); [Rödl & Partner](https://roedl.com/insights/ki-systeme-arbeitsverhaeltnis-betriebsrat); [Covington, July 2026 (PDF, not opened)](https://www.cov.com/-/media/files/corporate/publications/2026/07/works-council-rights-in-ai-implementation-projects.pdf)
- **EU AI Act Art. 26(7).** Deployers who are employers "shall inform workers' representatives and the affected workers" before putting a high-risk system into use at work. [Primary text via mirror] — [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)

**Different retention periods**
- EU deployers must keep high-risk logs "of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in applicable Union or national law" (Art. 26(6)). Authorised representatives keep documentation for 10 years (Art. 22(3)(b)). India CERT-In requires 180 days in India, and the DPDP Rules reportedly 1 year (above). Colorado S.B. 189 keeps three-year deployer records (trends.md §2.5). [Mixed; see the individual citations] — [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/); [Art. 22](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/22/)

**Different review or appeal rights for the same decision**
- EU Art. 26(11): deployers of Annex III systems that "make decisions or assist in making decisions related to natural persons shall inform the natural persons". Colorado S.B. 189 keeps rights to access, correct and request human review (trends.md §2.5). [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)

### Inferences
- The pattern behind every data-localisation case is to keep raw prompts and outputs that contain personal data in-country, and send the central function a record without personal data (decision, control result, reviewer, timestamp) plus a pointer. China's FAQ treats local access by travelling staff as no transfer, which also allows a central reviewer to review on site. Vietnam's six-monthly TIA refresh and the CAC's bimonthly filing notices show that the transfer basis itself has to be kept up to date.
- The Nanterre (suspended) and Paris (no consultation for Copilot) rulings show the trigger is the effect on working conditions, not the technology. A central roll-out calendar therefore needs a per-country "consultation complete" gate, including for pilots in France.
- A single global retention rule must be set to the longest period that applies, and then deletion must be reconciled with local minimums. This calls for a per-record retention attribute rather than one global retention setting.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the China Cybersecurity Law log-retention rule (I believe at least six months for network logs; unsourced here) or any explicit China rule on retaining AI-service logs.
- I found no published firm account of how a named multinational resolved a China, India or Vietnam log-export conflict for AI monitoring.
- I found no verified multinational AI framework agreement with a works council. A search result mentioned an IBM agreement with a risk-class scheme, which I could not confirm.
- I did not verify the CAC's own notices, the official text of Korea's Enforcement Decree, or the text of the Nanterre orders.

## 4. Who is "the human": local versus central reviewers, competence and AI literacy, authority to override, separation of duties, follow-the-sun review, language

### Takeaway
The EU AI Act sets the most concrete specification of who the human is. The person must have "competence, training and authority". They must be able to understand the system, resist automation bias, interpret its output, override or reverse it, and stop it. For one category, two separately competent people must confirm before action. Financial regulators (MAS, EIOPA) add role definition and contingency plans. In firms, the human is usually a spoke: a business-unit focal point or council member, with escalation to a central board. I found no sourced material on follow-the-sun review or on language requirements for reviewers.

### Cited Findings
- **Competence and authority (EU Art. 26(2)).** "Deployers shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority". [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)
- **What the human must be able to do (EU Art. 14(4)).** (a) "properly understand the relevant capacities and limitations" and monitor for "anomalies, dysfunctions and unexpected performance"; (b) remain aware of "automation bias"; (c) "correctly interpret the high-risk AI system's output"; (d) "decide, in any particular situation, not to use the high-risk AI system or to otherwise disregard, override or reverse the output"; (e) "intervene ... or interrupt the system through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state". [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)
- **Separation of duties in law (EU Art. 14(5)).** For Annex III point 1(a) (remote biometric identification), "no action or decision is taken by the deployer on the basis of the identification ... unless that identification has been separately verified and confirmed by at least two natural persons with the necessary competence, training and authority". There are carve-outs for law enforcement, migration and border uses. [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)
- **AI literacy softened, not removed.** The Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) reportedly dropped the "sufficient level" wording in Art. 4 and added Commission and Member State support duties. The Commission's Q&A reportedly says literacy remains an obligation for providers and deployers. Sources differ on the operative verb ("promote" or "support the development of"), and Art. 4 is not in the Art. 99(4) fine tier. [Search-summary; check EUR-Lex] — [KI-Campus](https://ki-campus.org/en/blog/eu-ai-act-ai-omnibus-recalibrates-article-4-ai-literacy-remains-mandatory); [Presencis](https://cdn.presencis.com/regulations/eu-ai-act/article-4/)
- **Singapore (final Guidelines, Oct 2026).** Human oversight is one of the lifecycle controls ("data management, human oversight, selection and testing"). High-risk use cases need contingency plans with fallback options. "An FI retains primary accountability for its use of third-party AI." [Secondary, opened] — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)
- **EU insurance (EIOPA Opinion, 6 Aug 2025).** No new requirements. It expects risk-based, proportionate governance and stresses human oversight, with roles clearly split among compliance, actuarial, data-protection and senior-management functions, under Solvency II, IDD and DORA. [Search-summary] — [EIOPA press release](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eiopa-publishes-opinion-ai-governance-and-risk-management-2025-08-06_en); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/law-in-tech/2025/eiopa-publishes-opinion-on-ai-governance-and-risk-management)
- **Understanding gap (UK FS, 2024).** 46% of firms reported only "partial understanding" of the AI they use, against 34% reporting "complete understanding", attributed largely to third-party models. A third of use cases are third-party implementations, up from 17% in 2022. 55% of use cases involve some automated decision-making, and 2% are fully autonomous. [Primary] — [BoE/FCA 2024](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024)
- **Who reviews in firms.** IBM's business-unit focal points triage first and escalate high-risk cases to the central Board ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/a-look-into-ibms-ai-ethics-governance-framework)) [Search-summary]. Standard Chartered's council chair, the Group CDO, approves models before deployment ([The Digital Banker](https://thedigitalbanker.com/making-ai-adoption-safe-how-standard-chartered-is-putting-humans-in-control-of-ai/)) [Search-summary]. Unilever escalates complex cases to external experts ([MIT SMR](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/ai-ethics-at-unilever-from-policy-to-process)) [Search-summary].

### Inferences
- In the published examples, the reviewer of an individual decision sits close to the business and the country, while the authority to set thresholds and approve high-risk cases sits centrally. Art. 26(2) requires the deployer to name competent people, and nothing in the cited text requires them to be in a given country. Locality comes from data-access constraints (section 3) and works-council or language needs, not from the AI Act.
- Art. 14(4)(d)–(e) provide concrete actions a demo can show: decline to use, override, reverse, stop. Art. 14(5) shows a two-person rule as a legal requirement, not just good practice.
- Third-party AI widens the gap: one third of UK FS use cases are third-party, and 46% of firms report only partial understanding. Reviewers are asked to have "competence" over systems their firm does not fully understand.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced material on follow-the-sun review of AI decisions, staffing of review queues across time zones, or reviewer language requirements, from regulators or firms.
- I found no survey figure on the share of firms whose human reviewers have authority to override, as distinct from escalating.
- Korea's and China's human-oversight requirements for high-impact or generative AI were not researched in detail.

## 5. Tracking regulatory change and turning it into control changes: horizon scanning, counts, cadence

### Takeaway
Volume counts exist (see trends.md for NCSL, OECD and Stanford). This session added the CAC's bimonthly filing cadence, Vietnam's six-monthly re-assessment cycle, and the MAS timeline from consultation to final Guidelines to effect. I found no published figure on how fast firms turn a regulatory change into a control change.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford AI Index 2025 (covers 2024; older).** AI mentions in legislative proceedings across 75 countries reached 1,889. Spain led with 314. The 2024 edition reported mentions in 49 countries rising from 1,247 to 2,175 (different country set). The 2026 edition's legislative figure was not found, as in trends.md §4.6. [Search-summary] — [Stanford HAI, 2025 AI Index policy chapter](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2025-ai-index-report/policy-and-governance)
- **IAPP Global AI Law and Policy Tracker.** The May 2025 edition reportedly covered 25 jurisdictions on six continents (24 in Oct 2024). A January 2026 edition and a February 2026 update exist, and their counts were not found. The tracker notes its map "does not represent the extent to which jurisdictions around the world are active". [Search-summary] — [AI Governance Library summary](https://www.aigl.blog/global-ai-law-and-policy-tracker-2/); [IAPP tracker PDF](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/bltd4dd5b2d705252bc/blt34a8e3844fb44942/global_ai_law_policy_tracker.pdf)
- **CAC cadence.** Generative-AI filing notices come out roughly every two months (Jan–Feb, Mar–Apr, May–Jun, Jul–Aug 2026), with 124 services added in July–August 2026 alone. [Search-summary] — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/43685-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-july-to-august-2026)
- **Vietnam cadence.** TIA and DPIA records must be updated every six months or within 10 days of significant change. - **MAS timeline.** The consultation (P017-2025) opened 13 Nov 2025 and closed 31 Jan 2026. Final Guidelines were published 7 Oct 2026, take effect 7 Oct 2027, and phase sections 5–6 to 7 Oct 2028. [Secondary, opened / search-summary] — [MAS consultation](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/consultations/2025/consultation-paper-on-guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-risk-management); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)
- **Standard Chartered** is refreshing its framework for generative AI, agentic AI and open-source models. [Search-summary] — [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/standard-chartered-s-risk-first-approach-to-scaling-ai-globally)
- **AstraZeneca's embedding time** was over six months to formulate principles plus a year to embed them across the business. [Primary, 2022] — [Mökander & Floridi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9152664/)

### Inferences
- A ten-country firm faces at least one recurring cadence per regime (CAC bimonthly, Vietnam six-monthly, EU at least 6-month log windows) on top of one-off changes such as the Omnibus delay, Colorado's repeal and the new MAS Guidelines. In 2026 alone the sources here show three of a ten-country firm's regimes changing materially (EU, Colorado, Singapore). This is a count across sources, not a figure from any one survey.
- AstraZeneca's roughly 18 months to embed principles, set against the MAS gap of about 11 months from consultation to final text, suggests that hand-run embedding is slower than rule changes. This is a comparison of single cases, not a measured rate.

### Gaps
- I found no published survey of time from regulatory change to control change, or of horizon-scanning staffing, for AI.
- Current IAPP tracker jurisdiction count and Stanford 2026 legislative counts were not found.
- No vendor-independent data on regulatory-change-management tools in AI governance.

## 6. Named organisations' programmes and regulator findings on cross-border AI governance

### Takeaway
Companies describe structures (committees, councils, focal points, assurance services) far more than evidence. The one independent, in-depth account is the 2021 AstraZeneca audit. It found no technical tests of AI systems, uneven awareness across business areas, and difficulty auditing vendor systems. Regulator surveys (BoE/FCA, MAS) cover governance inside a country. The only explicit cross-border regulator position found is MAS's Oct 2026 permission to rely on group frameworks.

### Cited Findings
- **AstraZeneca audit details.** The audit was coordinated by internal audit and conducted by an unnamed external professional-services firm. It ran 14 weeks and took about 2,000 person-hours, with a governance-level audit plus in-depth audits of selected projects. No technical tests of AI models were done. Vendor-supplied systems were hard to audit for lack of visibility into external data, and the authors recommend ethics criteria in procurement. The authors state process audits cannot "produce verifiable claims" about long-term effects of self-learning systems. [Primary, 2022] — [Mökander & Floridi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9152664/)
- **Microsoft.** The 2025 report covers pre-deployment review of high-impact uses and of "every flagship model added to the Azure OpenAI Service and every Phi model release". Microsoft says cross-border regulatory practices "are still being defined". [Primary] — [Microsoft blog, June 2025](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2025/06/20/our-2025-responsible-ai-transparency-report/). In 2024, 77% of Sensitive Uses consultations related to generative AI. [Search-summary] — [2025 report PDF](https://cdn-dynmedia-1.microsoft.com/is/content/microsoftcorp/microsoft/msc/documents/presentations/CSR/2025-Responsible-AI-Transparency-Report.pdf). The 2026 report reportedly cites 450+ Sensitive Use reviews and nearly 2,500 generative-AI product and feature reviews (July 2025–June 2026). [Secondary summary only] — [AI Governance Library](https://www.aigl.blog/responsible-ai-transparency-report-2026/)
- **HSBC, Standard Chartered, IBM, Unilever:** see section 1. All are self-described or trade-press accounts, not independently evidenced.
- **Bank of England/FCA 2024 (118 firms).** Top three providers hold 73% of cloud, 44% of model and 33% of data provision. 62% of use cases are low materiality and 16% high. Cybersecurity is the top perceived systemic risk, and the biggest expected rise is in critical third-party dependencies. [Primary] — [BoE/FCA 2024](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024)
- **MAS 2024 thematic review** (mid-2024, banks): most banks kept a formal AI inventory, used materiality to scale controls, and focused on controls ensuring AI works as intended in production. [Search-summary] — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/banking-finance_1/singapore-information-paper-on-ai-model-risk-management-in-financial-institutions); [MAS page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/monographs-or-information-paper/2024/artificial-intelligence-model-risk-management)
- **MAS final Guidelines (Oct 2026).** Inventory must be "accurate" and at "an appropriate level of granularity". Third-party contracts must give visibility of AI introductions and updates. FIs must test third-party AI on their own use cases and data and document it, and consider "limiting, suspending, or replacing a provider" outside risk appetite. A group framework may be leveraged. [Secondary, opened] — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)
- **EIOPA** (Aug 2025): supervisory expectations without new rules (section 4). [Search-summary] — [EIOPA](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/eiopa-publishes-opinion-ai-governance-and-risk-management-2025-08-06_en)

### Inferences
- MAS's final Guidelines amount to an operating spec a central function can show: inventory, materiality, lifecycle controls including human oversight, third-party test evidence, and contingency plans. They also explicitly allow a group framework, which is the strongest regulator text found for one central function serving local entities.
- The AstraZeneca findings (no technical tests, process-only, vendor opacity) and the BoE figures (one third of use cases third-party, 46% partial understanding) point to the same gap: central oversight that checks process documents but not system behaviour.

### Gaps
- Not researched in this session: JPMorgan Chase, ING, Novartis, Roche, Siemens and Salesforce programmes, ECB, and the FSB's 2025 AI monitoring report.
- I did not find any regulator finding specifically on how firms govern AI across borders, other than MAS's group-framework clause.
- I could not open the text of HSBC's and Standard Chartered's annual-report AI sections (search returned only contents pages).

## 7. Maturity surveys, reported gaps (shadow AI, agents, missing inventories) and evidence practices that serve several regulators at once

### Takeaway
Surveys agree that adoption outpaces governance, but their definitions differ and are not comparable. About 77% of organisations are "working on" AI governance (IAPP), 21% have a mature governance model for agentic AI (Deloitte), and 37% have policies to manage AI or detect shadow AI (IBM breach study). On evidence, the strongest finding is in the EU AI Act text itself: for financial institutions, existing financial-services governance and documentation count as meeting the Act's QMS, monitoring and log-keeping duties. That is one record serving two regimes. I found no guidance that one evidence record satisfies several countries' regulators.

### Cited Findings
**Maturity and gaps**
- **IAPP AI Governance Profession Report 2025** (published 16 Apr 2025; survey spring 2024, 671 respondents in 45 countries, so older data). 77% of organisations are working on AI governance: "near 90%" of AI users and 30% of non-users (the infographic says "over 85%", which conflicts). Primary owner of AI governance: privacy 22%, legal and compliance 22%, IT 17%, data governance 10%, ethics and compliance 6%, security 5%. Only 10 of 671 (1.5%) expect to need no extra staff. 23.5% cite finding qualified AI professionals as a challenge. [Primary] — [IAPP report page](https://iapp.org/resources/article/ai-governance-profession-report); [IAPP at-a-glance](https://iapp.org/resources/article/at-a-glance-ai-governance-profession-report-2025)
- **Deloitte State of AI in the Enterprise 2026** (3,235 leaders, 24 countries, Aug–Sept 2025). 21% report a mature governance model for agentic AI. About 23% currently use agents at least moderately, and 74% expect to by 2027. 73% name data privacy and security as their top AI risk. [Search-summary] — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/topics/emerging-technologies/ai-agents-scaling-faster.html); [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/deloitte-survey-finds-enterprises-charging-ahead-with-ai-agents-while-governance-struggles-to-keep-up)
- **EY Responsible AI Pulse** (975 C-suite in 21 countries; wave 1 fieldwork Mar–Apr 2025, wave 2 Aug–Sept 2025). On average organisations have strong controls in only three of nine facets. In the UK release, nearly 98% of UK respondents reported financial losses from unmanaged AI risks, averaging US$3.9m. [Search-summary] — [EY, June 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/newsroom/2025/06/ey-survey-ai-adoption-outpaces-governance-as-risk-awareness-among-the-c-suite-remains-low); [EY UK, Oct 2025](https://www.ey.com/en_uk/newsroom/2025/10/responsible-ai-governance-business-performance)
- **Gartner** (press release 17 Feb 2026; survey of 360 organisations, Q2 2025). Organisations that deployed AI governance platforms were "3.4 times more likely to achieve high effectiveness in AI governance". This is a correlation in a vendor-category analyst survey. [Search-summary] — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-17-gartner-global-ai-regulations-fuel-billion-dollar-market-for-ai-governance-platforms)
- **Shadow AI (IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025, Ponemon for IBM, vendor-commissioned).** About 20% of organisations reported a breach involving shadow AI, adding about US$670k to breach cost. Only 37% had policies to manage AI or detect shadow AI (so about 63% did not), and 34% audit for unsanctioned AI. Shadow-AI incidents compromised customer PII in 65% of cases against 53% overall. Secondary sources say nearly all AI-related breaches were at organisations lacking proper AI access controls; check IBM's exact figure. [Search-summary] — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-ai-breach-risks-rise-as-governance-lags/); [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/ten-key-insights-from-ibms-cost-of-a-data-breach-report-2025)
- **KPMG and University of Melbourne Trust in AI 2025** (48,340 people in 47 countries, Nov 2024–Jan 2025). 46% trust AI tools. 47% admit using AI in ways that could be deemed inappropriate, and about two-thirds rely on output without checking accuracy (general public, not employees). [Search-summary] — [University of Melbourne record](https://figshare.unimelb.edu.au/articles/report/Trust_attitudes_and_use_of_artificial_intelligence_A_global_study_2025/28822919); [Unleash](https://www.unleash.ai/artificial-intelligence/news/66-of-individuals-may-be-using-ai-but-79-are-wary-of-possible-risks-finds-kpmg)
- **Inventories.** Most MAS-reviewed banks had a formal AI inventory (2024) [Search-summary] ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/29565/mas-publishes-information-paper-on-ai-model-risk-management)), and MAS now requires an "accurate" inventory [Secondary, opened] ([Allen & Gledhill 2026](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)). Standard Chartered's AI Safety Council owns the inventory [Search-summary] ([The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/standard-chartered-s-risk-first-approach-to-scaling-ai-globally)).
- See trends.md §7.2 for IBM/Censuswide (EMEA, Sept 2026): 37% say governance is designed to scale with agentic AI.

**Evidence that serves several regimes**
- **QMS integration (EU Art. 17(3)).** Providers subject to quality-management obligations under sectoral Union law "may include the aspects listed in paragraph 1 as part of the quality management systems pursuant to that law". [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 17](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/17/)
- **Financial-institution deeming (EU Art. 17(4)).** For providers that are financial institutions, "the obligation to put in place a quality management system ... shall be deemed to be fulfilled by complying with the rules on internal governance arrangements or processes pursuant to the relevant Union financial services law". This excludes 17(1)(g)–(i): risk management, post-market monitoring and incident reporting. [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 17](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/17/)
- **Monitoring and logs (EU Art. 26(5)–(6)).** For financial-institution deployers, the monitoring obligation "shall be deemed to be fulfilled" by complying with internal-governance rules. They "shall maintain the logs as part of the documentation kept pursuant to the relevant Union financial service law". [Primary text via mirror] — [Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)
- **Cross-border reliance (Singapore).** An FI may leverage its group's AI risk framework (section 1). [Secondary, opened] — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption)
- **Third-party test evidence.** MAS expects FIs to test third-party AI on their own use cases and data and keep documentation of that testing. [Secondary, opened] — same source.
- **Duplication is resisted (AstraZeneca).** Ethics-based auditing works best "integrated with existing structures". Procedures that duplicate them "may be perceived as unnecessary". [Primary] — [Mökander & Floridi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9152664/)
- **Point-in-time documents are not verified in practice.** See trends.md §2.8: in the NYC LL144 audit, regulators found 1 issue where auditors found 17 or more.

### Inferences
- A per-decision record that carries the fields both a sectoral supervisor and the AI Act ask for (system, version, input reference, output, human reviewer with role and authority, override or stop action, timestamp, retention class, data-residency location) is the clearest mechanism that "serves several regulators at once". Art. 17(4) and 26(5)–(6) show the EU accepting the sectoral record for AI Act purposes, but only within the EU and for financial institutions.
- Combining section 3 with this section, one evidence record cannot be stored in one place for all jurisdictions. It has to be a record with location attributes, where the central function holds the record without personal data and each country holds the raw data.
- The survey figures show governance programmes are common (77% "working on" governance) while working controls are not (21% mature for agents, 37% with shadow-AI policies, strong controls in 3 of 9 EY facets). The definitions differ, so present them as separate facts, not a trend line.

### Gaps
- I found no regulator or standards-body guidance stating that one evidence record satisfies several countries' regimes. The EU deeming provisions are intra-EU and sectoral only.
- The McKinsey State of AI 2025 (Nov 2025) governance figures (CEO oversight, inventories, human-in-the-loop share) were not obtained. trends.md §6.4 has its adoption figures only.
- Global EY loss figures (as opposed to UK), Gartner TRiSM percentages and the full KPMG workplace figures (concealed use, uploads of company data) were not verified.
- I found no survey figure on the share of multinationals with a single central AI inventory covering all countries.
