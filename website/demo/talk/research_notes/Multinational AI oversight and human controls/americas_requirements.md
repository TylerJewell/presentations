Confidence labels on each finding: **[P]** = primary text opened and read this session; **[S]** = secondary source (law firm, news, tracker) opened this session; **[SS]** = search-result summary only, page not opened; do not put on a slide without opening it. ## 1. US federal: EO 14179, the AI Action Plan, OMB M-25-21/M-25-22, EO 14365 and the 2026 status of pre-emption and litigation

### Takeaway
The only binding federal human-oversight rules are for federal agencies' own AI: OMB M-25-21 (3 Apr 2025) requires, for "high-impact" AI, pre-deployment testing, an impact assessment with independent review and a signed risk acceptance, ongoing monitoring with periodic human review, operator training, "human oversight, intervention, and accountability", and "timely human review and a chance to appeal", with non-compliant uses to be discontinued after 365 days (3 Apr 2026). For private actors, federal policy runs the other way: EO 14365 and the 20 Mar 2026 White House framework push to pre-empt state AI laws, but no pre-emption statute has passed, Commerce's list of "onerous" state laws could not be confirmed as published, and the only federal courtroom move found is DOJ's April 2026 intervention in xAI's suit against Colorado.

### Cited Findings
**EO 14179 and M-25-21 (federal agencies' own AI) — enacted policy, binding on agencies**
- M-25-21, "Accelerating Federal Use of AI through Innovation, Governance, and Public Trust", is dated 3 April 2025, implements EO 14179 (signed 23 Jan 2025, "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in AI"), and "rescinds and replaces" OMB M-24-10. [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Definition (Sec. 4(a), Sec. 5): AI is high-impact "when its output serves as a principal basis for decisions or actions that have a legal, material, binding, or significant effect on rights or safety"; "A high-impact determination is possible whether there is or is not human oversight for the decision or action." Sec. 6 lists categories presumed high-impact, including medical-device functions and diagnosis, public-insurance care allocation, law-enforcement risk assessments and biometric ID, immigration risk assessments, applications for "critical federal services, processes, and benefits to include loans and access to public housing", and terms of federal employment (hiring, termination, discipline). An official must document in writing to the Chief AI Officer (CAIO) any decision that a presumed use is not high-impact. [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Deadline and termination (Sec. 4(a)(i)): "Within 365 days of the issuance of this memorandum, agencies must document implementation of the minimum practices ... If a particular high-impact use case is not compliant with the minimum practices then the agency must safely discontinue use of the AI functionality." 365 days from 3 Apr 2025 is 3 Apr 2026 (arithmetic mine). Pilots are exempt if limited, CAIO-certified, and, "when possible", participants can opt in and out. [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Waivers (Sec. 4(a)(ii)-(iv)): only the CAIO may waive a practice, in writing, on a system-specific risk assessment; waivers are re-certified annually, reported to OMB within 30 days, and summarised publicly. [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Minimum practices (Sec. 4(b)(i)-(vii)), quoted: (i) "Conduct Pre-Deployment Testing" (if no access to code, models or data, use "alternative test methodologies, such as querying the AI service and observing the outputs"); (ii) "Complete AI Impact Assessment" before deployment, updated through the lifecycle, covering purpose, data, impacts, "reassessment scheduling ... following significant modifications", costs, "results of independent review ... by an independent reviewer within the agency who has not been involved in the development", and "risk acceptance, supported by a signature from the individual accepting the risk"; (iii) "agencies must conduct testing and periodic human review of AI use cases, where feasible", with monitoring "designed to detect ... changes to an AI system after deployment, or changes to the context of use"; (iv) "sufficient and periodic training, assessment, and oversight for operators of AI to interpret and act on the AI's output"; (v) "Agencies must ensure human oversight, intervention, and accountability suitable for high-impact use cases ... an appropriate fail-safe that minimizes the risk of significant harm" (footnote 31 accepts automated safety actions where human approval is impracticable, but oversight and accountability must still be determined); (vi) "individuals affected by AI-enabled decisions have access to a timely human review and a chance to appeal any negative impacts, when appropriate"; (vii) an option for end users and the public to submit feedback. [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Governance cadence (Consolidated Table of Actions): CAIO within 60 days; AI governance board within 90 days; compliance plan within 180 days and every two years until 2036; public AI use-case inventory annually (DoD exempt from case-level inventory). [P] — [OMB M-25-21](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
- Compliance in practice: FedScoop reportedly found several agencies missed the April 2026 deadline; one source says the 180-day inventory deadline (Sept 2025) was met unevenly; there are no financial penalties. A Forkast article claims the White House page hosting M-25-21 returned 404, but the PDF downloaded from whitehouse.gov on 31 Oct 2026. Reddit snapshots mention later memos M-26-04 and M-26-05; content not verified. [SS] — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-federal-ai-compliance-deadline-is-here-the-memo-behind-it-has-vanished/); [CFPB compliance plan, Sept 2025](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_ai-compliance-plan_2025-09.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a multinational, M-25-21 matters as a template and as a contract flow-down when selling to US agencies (the Sec. 4(b)(i) "querying the AI service" test method assumes vendor-hosted models); it does not bind private deployments.
- M-25-21's seven practices map closely onto what private regimes now ask (testing, impact assessment, monitoring, trained operators, human intervention, appeal, feedback), so a central oversight programme can use it as the US federal baseline.
- Federal pre-emption remains a threat, not a rule: state human-review duties (Colorado, California) are in force or scheduled, and the framework itself proposes to leave generally applicable consumer-protection laws alone.

## 2. US states: Colorado, California, Texas, Illinois, New York City and State, Utah, and 2026 human-review laws

### Takeaway
State law is where enforceable private-sector human-review rights live. Colorado's SB 26-189 (signed 14 May 2026, effective 1 Jan 2027) and California's CPPA ADMT rules (compliance 1 Jan 2027) both give a right to a human reviewer who can change the decision; California's new SB 947 (operative 1 Jul 2027) requires human corroboration before discipline or termination that primarily relies on an automated system. In 2026 at least eight states barred health insurers from relying solely on AI for adverse coverage decisions. Texas TRAIGA and Illinois HB 3773 took effect 1 Jan 2026 but impose prohibitions and notice rather than human review, and Illinois' notice rules were withdrawn.

### Cited Findings
**Colorado — enacted; effective 1 Jan 2027; rules pending; litigation stayed**
- SB 26-189 (Automated Decision-Making Technology Act) signed 14 May 2026 by Gov. Polis; repeals and replaces SB 24-205; applies to consequential decisions on or after 1 Jan 2027. [S] — [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law); bill page (not opened): [leg.colorado.gov SB26-189](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189)
- Scope: "Covered ADMT" is ADMT that "materially influences" a consequential decision about access, eligibility, selection, compensation or materially worse terms in education, employment, financial or lending services, insurance, healthcare, and essential government services/public benefits. Exclusions include spreadsheets, summarisation and translation tools, fraud prevention, AML/sanctions, identity verification and cybersecurity. "Consumer" includes Colorado employees and applicants. [S] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law)
- Human-oversight duties: pre-use notice that covered ADMT is used and how to request human review; post-adverse-outcome notice within 30 days explaining the decision and the ADMT's role; rights to access and correct inaccurate personal data; and a right to request "human review and reconsideration" "to the extent commercially reasonable", by a reviewer who has authority to approve, modify or override, considers primary evidence, is trained, does "not simply defer to the automated output", and understands the system's intended use, limitations, inputs and principal factors. Developers must supply documentation including "instructions for appropriate use, monitoring, and human review". Records kept at least three years. [S] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law)
- Enforcement: Attorney General exclusively, under the Colorado Consumer Protection Act; no private right of action; notice of violation first; 60-day cure (not for knowing or repeated violations) sunsetting 1 Jan 2030; penalties up to US$20,000 per violation per Skadden (trends.md) and other trackers. Insurers subject to Colorado's insurance algorithm law are generally deemed compliant; HIPAA entities largely exempt. [S] — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/05/colorado-ai-act-repeal-new-transparency-law); [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-ai-act)
- AG rulemaking (4 CCR 904-6): proposed rules published 11 Aug 2026; revised draft about 6-20 Oct 2026; hearing and comment deadline 26 Oct 2026; rules mandatory by 1 Jan 2027 (content and format of adverse-outcome disclosures). Officials reportedly said a final draft would follow within about two months; final adoption not confirmed. [SS] — [Moore & Van Allen](https://www.mvalaw.com/data-points/colorados-proposed-admt-rules-what-businesses-need-to-know-now); [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2026/08/12/colorado-publishes-draft-admt-regulations/); [CSA research note, 20 Oct 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-colorado-admt-chatbot-rulemaking-20261020/)

**California — several enacted instruments**

- §7200(b): "A business that uses ADMT for a significant decision prior to January 1, 2027, must be in compliance ... no later than January 1, 2027"; new uses must comply from first use. [P] — [CPPA approved text](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/ccpa_updates_cyber_risk_admt_appr_text.pdf)

### Inferences
- The two state human-review definitions (Colorado: reviewer can override, considers evidence, does not defer; California: reviewer can interpret output and has authority to change the decision) are close enough to run as one control with one reviewer record, starting 1 Jan 2027.
- California's §7001(e) cuts both ways: a business that inserts a qualified human with real authority before the decision takes the system outside "ADMT" entirely. That makes the reviewer's competence and authority the evidence a regulator will ask for.
- Health-insurance utilisation review is the most widespread US human-in-the-loop mandate in 2026, and it targets agentic claims and prior-authorisation automation directly.

## 3. US sector rules: banking model risk, CFPB, EEOC, FDA, insurance (NAIC and Colorado), HHS/ONC

## 4. Canada: AIDA's death, the federal stance since, OSFI E-23, Quebec section 12.1, Ontario, and the Directive on Automated Decision-Making

### Takeaway
Canada still has no AI statute. The federal vehicle for human oversight is now privacy law: Bill C-36 (tabled 15 June 2026, at second reading) would give a right to an explanation of automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effect and a right to make written representations to an employee who can review the decision. Quebec already requires that, for decisions based exclusively on automated processing, under section 12.1 (in force since September 2023). OSFI E-23 (effective 1 May 2027) covers AI/ML models at federally regulated financial institutions with risk ratings that factor in autonomy. Ontario's job-posting AI disclosure took effect 1 Jan 2026, and the federal Directive on Automated Decision-Making's legacy-system transition ended 24 June 2026.

## 5. Brazil: PL 2338/2023, LGPD Article 20, and ANPD

### Takeaway
PL 2338/2023 passed the Senate on 10 Dec 2024 and, on 31 Oct 2026, sits in the Chamber's special committee "Aguardando Parecer do Relator" with no report or vote recorded. If enacted as the Senate wrote it, high-risk systems would carry rights to explanation, to contest, and to human review (Art. 6), human supervision so people can "compreender, interpretar, decidir e intervir" (Art. 8), documentation of the degree of human supervision (Art. 18), pre-market and lifecycle algorithmic impact assessments (Arts. 25-26), ANPD coordination of the national system (Art. 45), fines up to R$50 million or 2% of Brazilian revenue (Art. 50), and a general 730-day vacatio. Today the binding rule is LGPD Art. 20, a right to request review of solely automated decisions; the requirement that review be by a natural person was vetoed in 2019.

## 7. Enforcement actions to date

### Takeaway
Enforcement that specifically demands human review is thin. The record is mostly deception and accuracy cases (FTC, Texas AG), one FTC facial-recognition ban with deletion orders (Rite Aid), the FTC reversing an AI order in December 2025 on the Action Plan's instruction, Brazil's ANPD briefly halting Meta's AI training in 2024, and weak NYC LL144 enforcement. No TRAIGA, Colorado, CPPA-ADMT or Illinois HB 3773 enforcement action was found.

### Cited Findings
- FTC v. Rite Aid (Dec 2023, proposed stipulated order): five-year ban on facial recognition for surveillance; deletion of biometric data and notice to third parties to delete; required a monitoring programme, consumer notice, retention schedule, and annual CEO certification; complaint faulted lack of pre-deployment accuracy testing, monitoring and employee training. A human-review mandate in the order was not found. [SS] — [Mayer Brown](https://mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2023/12/more-than-a-ban-on-facial-recognition-use-the-us--ftcs-rite-aid-action-and-proposed-stipulated-order); [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/82185)
- FTC set aside its 2024 Rytr consent order on 22 Dec 2025 (2-0, Ferguson and Meador), stating that it "unduly burdens innovation in the nascent AI industry", citing the AI Action Plan's directive to review FTC orders; the FTC says it will still pursue firms that "deceive consumers about the capabilities of their generative AI". [SS] — [Covington Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/01/ftc-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-pursuant-to-white-house-ai-action-plan/); [Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/54731/2026-02-13-emerging-federal-ai-strategy-ftc-sets-aside-rytr)
- Texas AG v. Pieces Technologies (announced 18 Sept 2024): settlement over claimed "severe hallucination rate" of "<1 per 100,000"; no payment; future accuracy disclosures required; first state AG generative-AI healthcare settlement. Pre-TRAIGA, under the Texas DTPA. [SS] — [Texas AG release](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-reaches-settlement-first-its-kind-healthcare-generative-ai-investigation); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20241010-texas-attorney-ags-office-reaches-settlement-with-ai-company-over-deceptive-claims)
- ANPD v. Meta (Brazil): on 2 July 2024 ANPD ordered immediate suspension of Meta's privacy policy allowing use of personal data to train generative AI, with a daily fine of R$50,000; upheld on reconsideration in July; reversed on 30 Aug 2024 after Meta committed to a compliance plan and not to use data of under-18s while the inspection continued. [SS] — [CNN Brasil](https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/meta-e-proibida-de-usar-dados-de-usuarios-para-treinamento-de-inteligencia-artificial-no-instagram-e-facebook/); [Gazeta do Povo](https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/vida-e-cidadania/anpd-autoriza-meta-dados-brasileiros-inteligencia-artificial/)
- NYC LL144: the city agency found one issue among 32 companies where auditors found at least 17. [P via trends.md] — [NY State Comptroller](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)
- Texas TRAIGA: complaint portal live 1 Sept 2026; no enforcement action reported. [SS] — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-texas-traiga-enforcement-portal-20260913-c/)

### Inferences
- The enforcement lesson for a central oversight team is about evidence, not mandates: regulators have acted on untested accuracy claims, absent monitoring, untrained operators and missing deletion, which are the controls M-25-21, Colorado and PL 2338 now name.

### Gaps
- Earlier FTC algorithm-deletion orders (Everalbum 2021, WW/Kurbo 2022) and the 2024-2025 "Operation AI Comply" cases not re-verified this session.
- Whether the Rite Aid order was finalised by the bankruptcy and district courts not confirmed.
- ANPD actions on AI after 2024 (for example, biometric or chatbot cases) not found.
- Canadian enforcement (Quebec CAI decisions on s. 12.1, OPC) not found.
