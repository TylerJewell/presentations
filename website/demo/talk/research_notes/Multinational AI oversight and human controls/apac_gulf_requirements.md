# Human oversight and AI governance requirements in Asia-Pacific and the Gulf (as of 31 October 2026)

These notes were researched on 31 October 2026. They cover China, South Korea, Japan, Singapore, India, Australia, Vietnam, Taiwan, Hong Kong, the UAE and Saudi Arabia. Each finding carries a confidence tag in the same spirit as `demo/talk/research/trends.md`:
- **[P]** Primary: I opened and read the page in the issuing body's own publication.
- **[S]** Secondary, opened: I opened and read a law-firm, academic, news or government-tracker page.
- **[SS]** Search-summary only: the finding came from a search result summary and I did not open the page. Open it before putting it on a slide.

Each finding is also marked by status: **In force**, **Issued (not yet applicable)**, **Draft/Proposal**, or **Soft law** (voluntary guidance).

## China: algorithm, deep-synthesis and generative-AI rules, filing, labelling, the 2026 Cybersecurity Law amendment, human-like AI, AI agents and PIPL transfers

### Takeaway
China has the densest binding regime in the region. Public-facing generative AI must pass a security assessment and complete algorithm filing. By 31 August 2026, 1,112 generative AI services had been filed and 731 apps or features registered. Mandatory explicit and implicit labelling has applied since 1 September 2025. In 2026 China added binding rules for human-like "companion" AI (effective 15 July 2026) and a dedicated policy on AI agents (8 May 2026), which gives users the final decision on what an agent decides by itself. For central oversight, two points matter most. Remote viewing from abroad of data stored in China counts as an outbound transfer. And no data stored in China may go to a foreign court or enforcement body without Chinese approval.

### Cited Findings
**Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (In force since 15 Aug 2023)**
- The Measures are Order No. 15, issued by seven bodies (CAC, NDRC, MOE, MOST, MIIT, MPS, NRTA). The CAC approved them on 23 May 2023 and the order is dated 10 July 2023. They take effect on 15 August 2023 (Art. 24: "本办法自2023年8月15日起施行"). [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)
- Scope (Art. 2): generative AI services offered to the public in mainland China. An organisation that develops or uses generative AI without offering it to the domestic public is outside the Measures ("未向境内公众提供生成式人工智能服务的，不适用本办法的规定"). Internal enterprise use is therefore outside these Measures, though PIPL, the Data Security Law and the Cybersecurity Law still apply. [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)
- Art. 9: providers bear responsibility as producers of online information content and as personal-information handlers. Art. 12: generated images and video must be labelled under the Deep Synthesis Provisions. Art. 14: on finding illegal content, a provider must stop generation, remove the content, retrain the model and report to the authorities; on finding a user acting illegally, it must warn, restrict or suspend the user, keep records and report. Art. 15: providers must run a complaint channel with published procedures and deadlines. [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)
- Art. 17: services with "public opinion attributes or social mobilisation capacity" must complete a security assessment and algorithm filing, change and cancellation under the Algorithm Recommendation Provisions (《互联网信息服务算法推荐管理规定》). Art. 20: if a service provided from outside China does not comply, the CAC notifies other bodies to take "technical and other necessary measures" against it. Art. 21: penalties run under the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law and PIPL; otherwise warnings and correction orders, and suspension of the service if the provider refuses or the case is serious. [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm)

**Filing and registration counts**
- By 31 August 2026, 1,112 generative AI services had completed filing and 731 apps or features had completed registration. [SS] — [Digital Policy Alert, Jul–Aug 2026 batch](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/43685-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-july-to-august-2026); [remio](https://www.remio.ai/post/cac-generative-ai-filings-hit-1-112-as-china-speeds-up-compliance)
- The trajectory: 988 filed and 598 registered by 30 June 2026 ([DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/41563-cyberspace-administration-of-china-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-may-to-june-2026)); 796 and 481 by 28 February 2026 ([DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/38517-cyberspace-administration-of-china-announced-registered-generative-ai-service-information-for-january-to-february-2026)); 748 by the end of 2025 (446 added in 2025) and 302 by the end of 2024 ([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/technology/articles/2428775/china-s-generative-ai-registry-expands-as-filings-registrations-surge-in-2025)); "over 700" in December 2025 ([China Daily](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202512/26/WS694e1022498e23165e06c331/over-700-generative-ai-large-model-products-complete-filing-in-china.html)). Three more mobile-side services were filed on 23 September 2026 ([DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/44533-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-3-mobile-side-generative-ai-services)). [SS]
- There are two tracks. Model or service providers file with the CAC. Downstream apps that call an already-filed model register with the local cyberspace administration. Live apps must show the model name and its filing or launch number prominently. [SS] — [DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/43685-cyberspace-administration-issued-filing-information-for-generative-ai-services-for-july-to-august-2026)
- The CAC also publishes separate deep-synthesis algorithm filing lists. The 18th batch was dated 17 July 2026; the 16th was 12 March 2026 and the 17th was 6 May 2026. No cumulative count of filed algorithms was found. [SS] — [DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/41884-cyberspace-administration-published-eighteenth-batch-of-domestic-deep-synthesis-service-algorithm-filing-list)

**Measures for Labelling AI-Generated Synthetic Content (In force since 1 Sept 2025)**
- Notice 国信办通字〔2025〕2号 was issued by the CAC, MIIT, MPS and NRTA. It was signed on 7 March 2025, published on 14 March 2025 and took effect on 1 September 2025 (Art. 14). [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm)
- Art. 4: providers must add explicit labels to text, audio, images, video and virtual scenes, and downloads, copies and exports must keep them. Art. 5: providers must embed implicit metadata giving the content attributes, the provider's name or code and a content ID; watermarks are encouraged. Art. 6: platforms must check metadata, label confirmed, declared or suspected synthetic content and add propagation metadata. Art. 7: app stores must verify the labelling materials of AI apps. Art. 10: users must declare synthetic content, and no one may remove, alter or forge labels. Art. 12: labelling materials must be submitted with algorithm filing or security assessment. [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm)
- Art. 9 sets a records duty. A provider that supplies content without explicit labels at a user's request must keep logs of the recipients for at least six months ("留存提供对象信息等相关日志不少于六个月"). [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm)
- The mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025 took effect on the same date. [SS] — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/03/chinas-ai-labeling-measures-and-mandatory-national-standards-take-effect-september-1)

**Cybersecurity Law amendment (In force since 1 Jan 2026)**
- The NPC Standing Committee adopted the amendment on 28 October 2025 and it took effect on 1 January 2026. A new Article 20 says the state supports AI research and infrastructure, improves AI ethical norms and strengthens "risk monitoring, assessment and safety supervision". [SS] — [Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2026-01-08/china-amended-cybersecurity-law-takes-effect/); [China Daily](https://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/npc/2025-10/29/c_1136120.htm); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-10-29/china-amends-cybersecurity-law-to-add-first-ever-ai-clause-102376988.html)
- The amendment raises the general fine cap from RMB 1 million to RMB 10 million and extends the law's reach to conduct overseas. The AI article is framed as state promotion, not as company duties. [S] — [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-china-ai-agent-regulation-enforcement-2026/)

**Interim Measures for Anthropomorphic (human-like) AI Interaction Services (In force since 15 July 2026)**
- The Measures were issued on 10 April 2026 by the CAC, NDRC, MIIT, MPS and SAMR and took effect on 15 July 2026. The draft was open for comment from 27 December 2025 to 25 January 2026. [S] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/chinas-interim-measures-for-the-administration-of-anthropomorphic-ai-interaction-services); draft dates [SS] — [BABL AI](https://babl.ai/?p=9349)
- Scope (Art. 2): services that "simulate human personality traits, thinking patterns, and communication styles" to give users "continuous emotional interaction". Customer service, knowledge Q&A, workplace assistants, education and research tools are excluded. [S] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/chinas-interim-measures-for-the-administration-of-anthropomorphic-ai-interaction-services)
- Human intervention: for risks of self-harm, suicide or significant financial loss, providers must "implement tiered intervention measures", which may include contacting designated guardians or emergency contacts. [S] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/chinas-interim-measures-for-the-administration-of-anthropomorphic-ai-interaction-services)
- A security assessment is required at launch, after significant changes, and once a service passes "more than 1 million registered users or over 100,000 monthly active users". Algorithm filing and periodic verification are also required. Sensitive interaction data may not be used for training without separate, explicit consent. Virtual intimate-relationship services for minors are prohibited, and users under 14 need guardian consent. [S] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/chinas-interim-measures-for-the-administration-of-anthropomorphic-ai-interaction-services)
- Providers are also barred from fostering emotional dependency and from emotional manipulation that leads users into unreasonable decisions. [S] — [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/English/2026-07/28/content_554571.html)

**AI agents: Implementation Opinions on the Standardised Application and Innovative Development of Intelligent Agents (智能体规范应用与创新发展实施意见), issued 8 May 2026**
- Issued by the CAC, NDRC and MIIT. The Opinions define an agent as an "intelligent system capable of autonomous perception, memory, decision-making, interaction, and execution". [S] — [NYU Shanghai RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents)
- Human control: developers must set decision boundaries and the authority each decision requires, in three tiers (decided by the user only, requiring user authorisation, or autonomous by the agent). Users "have the right to know and the final decision-making power regarding the autonomous decisions made by the intelligent agent." [S] — [RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents)
- Agents in healthcare, transport, media and public safety face filing, mandatory testing and possible recalls. Lower-risk uses rely on platform governance, third-party evaluation and a credit system. [S] — [RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents)
- The sources conflict on binding force. The CSA says the Opinions became enforceable on 15 July 2026 but are "a policy framework rather than a self-contained enforcement statute", with fines expected in later guidance. RITS gives no effective date. [S] — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-china-ai-agent-regulation-enforcement-2026/); [RITS](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/china-issues-first-national-policy-framework-dedicated-to-ai-agents)
- The Opinions target 70% agent adoption in smart terminals by 2027. [SS] — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-12/china-issues-guidelines-to-standardize-ai-agent-development-102443370.html)
- Draft: on 30 July 2026 Chinese standard-setters published draft safety requirements for agents interacting with each other and with external tools, tied to a proposed mandatory national standard. [SS] — [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/articles/2507569/china-advances-ai-agent-governance-with-draft-security-guide-mandatory-standard)
- Soft law: the TC260 "Ethics-Safety Guidelines for AI Applications 1.0" took effect on 1 July 2026 and are voluntary. [S] — [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-china-ai-agent-regulation-enforcement-2026/)

**PIPL cross-border transfers (In force)**
- Three routes exist: the CAC security assessment, the standard contract, and certification. The Provisions on Promoting and Regulating Cross-Border Data Flows (in force since 22 March 2024) set the thresholds. A security assessment is needed above 1 million individuals' non-sensitive personal information, or above 10,000 individuals' sensitive information, counted cumulatively from 1 January. Between 100,000 and 1 million non-sensitive, or under 10,000 sensitive, a standard contract or certification is needed. Free-trade-zone negative lists can exempt transfers. [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/china-finalizes-its-provisions-to-promote-and-regulate-cross-border-data-transfers); [Han Kun](https://www.hankunlaw.com/en/portal/article/index/cid/8/id/14070.html)
- The Measures for Certification of Personal Information Outbound Transfer (CAC and SAMR; issued 14 or 17 October 2025, as sources differ) took effect on 1 January 2026. They cover non-CIIO processors in the 100,000 to 1 million band. The first three accredited bodies were named on 30 December 2025. [SS] — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/10/chinas-data-outbound-rules-update-measures-for-the-certification); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/11/china-requirements-personal-information-protection-certification)
- Remote access: under the 2022 Security Assessment Measures and a CAC Q&A, overseas entities or individuals accessing data stored in China count as an outbound transfer. A minority view disputes this under PIPL. [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/key-questions-answered-chinas-regulation-of-international-data-transfers-effective-1-september-2022); [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/china/security/1211326/china-officially-released-its-security-assessment-rules-for-cross-border-data-transfer)
- PIPL Art. 40: critical information infrastructure operators, and processors above a CAC-set volume, must store personal information inside China. Art. 41: without approval from a competent Chinese authority, no processor may provide personal information stored in China to foreign judicial or enforcement bodies. Cooley calls this a "blocking statute". [SS] — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/part-3-pipls-localization-requirements-and-restrictions-on-responding-to-foreign-judicial-and-enforcement-agencies/); [Davis Polk](https://davispolk.com/insights/client-update/chinas-personal-data-law-comes-force-accompanied-draft-rules-cross-border)

### Inferences
- A non-Chinese oversight team can review Chinese-user decisions only through a transfer route (standard contract, certification or assessment), because even remote viewing is an outbound transfer. The simplest design keeps raw transcripts and logs in China and exports aggregated, de-identified verdicts. This is my inference from the remote-access and threshold rules.
- The anthropomorphic Measures and the agent Opinions are the first APAC instruments to put a human-or-user decision point into the rules themselves: "tiered intervention" for companions, and user "final decision-making power" for agents.
- Purely internal enterprise agents are outside the generative AI Measures (Art. 2) but probably inside the agent Opinions' sector filing regime if they work in healthcare, transport, media or public safety. That second point is not confirmed.

### Gaps
- I did not open the CAC text of the agent Opinions or of the anthropomorphic Measures, so their article numbers, exact human-intervention wording and binding status are unverified. A CSA table (autonomous, human approval for reversible actions, automation prohibited for hard-to-reverse actions) is the CSA's own compilation, not CAC text.
- The effective dates of the Algorithm Recommendation Provisions (widely reported as 1 March 2022) and the Deep Synthesis Provisions (widely reported as 10 January 2023) were not opened this session.
- I found no cumulative count of filed algorithms across all lists.
- The current CAC threshold for Art. 40 localization by non-CIIO processors was not found.

## South Korea: Framework Act on AI (AI Basic Act), effective 22 January 2026

### Takeaway
The Act and its Enforcement Decree have been in force since 22 January 2026. Operators of high-impact AI must provide risk management, explanation, user protection and **human management and supervision**, and must keep documentation. Draft notices set five-year retention and a named responsible manager. Generative and high-impact AI require advance notice to users and labelling of outputs. Foreign firms above any one of three thresholds must appoint a domestic agent. Fines are capped at KRW 30 million and are deferred for a grace period of at least one year, so they start no earlier than about January 2027.

### Cited Findings
- The Act and the Enforcement Decree took effect on 22 January 2026; the grace period is "at least one year". During it, fact-finding investigations and administrative fines are "generally deferred, except in exceptional cases involving serious social harm, such as loss of life or human-rights violations". (US Commercial Service page dated 29 May 2026.) [S] — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)
- The Cabinet enacted the Enforcement Decree on 21 January 2026 [SS] — [Cooley (search result)](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways). It is Presidential Decree No. 36053 [SS] — [DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/37278-enforcement-decree-of-the-framework-act-on-the-development-of-artificial-intelligence-and-creation-of-a-trust-base-presidential-decree-no-36053-entered-into-force)
- High-impact AI is AI that "significantly affects human life, safety or fundamental rights", in sectors including healthcare, energy, transport, hiring and biometric analysis. Operators must self-assess before deployment and may ask MSIT to confirm. They must give a "meaningful explanation" of outcomes, the key criteria behind them and a summary of training data; deploy a user protection plan; "maintain a mechanism for human intervention and oversight"; document the measures taken; and "make efforts" to assess the impact on fundamental rights. [S] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- MSIT's guidance lists risk management plans, explainability, user safeguards, human oversight, documentation and recordkeeping for high-impact AI. [S] — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)
- The draft Business Responsibilities Notice under **Article 34** (September 2025 draft) defines human management and supervision as "defined intervention points, interruption and rollback mechanisms, inspection schedules, and staff training". The Decree requires a named responsible manager with contact details. Records of risk management, explanation, user protection and human oversight are kept for **five years** by default, and the main contents are disclosed publicly, with trade secrets redactable. [S, draft] — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102low8/south-korean-ministry-of-science-and-ict-issues-package-of-regulations-to-supplem)
- High-performance AI is AI trained with at least 10^26 FLOPs. It carries lifecycle risk management and reporting to MSIT; the draft deadlines are 3 months after confirmation, 1 month after material changes, 7 days after serious incidents, and annually. [S] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways); [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102low8/south-korean-ministry-of-science-and-ict-issues-package-of-regulations-to-supplem)
- Generative AI: operators of generative and high-impact AI must tell users in advance that the product uses AI. Generative outputs must be labelled, and realistic AI sound, images or video need clear notice [S] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways). Inside a service, notices can be flexible (UI notices, pop-ups); exported or downloadable content needs clearer labelling [S] — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)
- Domestic agent: a foreign operator with no Korean address or office must appoint one if it meets any of these tests: prior-year total revenue of at least KRW 1 trillion (about US$662–681 million); AI-service revenue of at least KRW 10 billion; or at least 1 million average daily Korean users over the three months before year-end. A firm fined for ignoring an MSIT suspension or corrective order may also have to appoint one. The agent answers government inquiries and safety reports. [S] — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- Fines of up to KRW 30 million (about US$21,000) apply for failing to notify users of AI use, failing to appoint a domestic agent, or breaching corrective orders or refusing inspections. MSIT can order a service suspended where it poses a safety threat. The Act applies extraterritorially to AI that affects the Korean market. [S] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways)
- The first amendment, consolidated from nine bills, passed on 30 or 31 December 2025. Reports of its entry into force conflict: one says 22 January 2027; another says an amending Act promulgated 20 January 2026 plus an amended Decree promulgated 20 July 2026. [SS, conflicting] — [MLex/Law360](https://www.law360.com/mlex/artificial-intelligence/articles/2425711/first-amendment-to-south-korea-s-ai-basic-act-approved)
- Further amendment bills on national-security high-risk models, children and anti-discrimination were under deliberation in 2026 and none had passed. [SS] — [DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/20769-bill-amending-ai-basic-act-to-establish-designation-and-management-systems-for-high-risk-ai-models-posing-threats-to-national-security-and-critical-infrastructure-bill-no-2220395)

### Inferences
- Korea is the only country in this set whose statute names "human management and supervision" as a standing duty of high-impact AI operators, backed by public disclosure of the oversight plan and a named manager. The central console has to be able to show a regulator, per system, who supervises it, where the intervention points are, and five years of records.
- The domestic agent is a legal point of contact, not a local oversight function, so oversight itself can sit abroad, subject to PIPA's transfer rules.

### Gaps
- I did not open the official English text on law.go.kr, so the article numbers for transparency (reported as Art. 31), the domestic agent (Art. 36) and fines (Art. 43) are unconfirmed. Only Art. 34 is confirmed, and only via Baker Botts.
- Whether the final notices kept the five-year retention and the named-manager rule from the September 2025 drafts is not confirmed.
- The content and effective date of the December 2025 amendment are unresolved.
- I did not research the PIPA cross-border rules in depth. Secondary sources list consent, PIPC adequacy, standard clauses and certification under PIPA Art. 28-8 [SS] — [Legal 500 / Lee & Ko](https://legal500.com/firms/30803-lee-ko/c-south-korea/news-and-developments/major-amendment-to-the-personal-information-protection-act-passed-by-national-assembly-ii). I found no general localization rule.

## Japan: AI Promotion Act (2025), AI Basic Plan, AI Guidelines for Business and FSA

### Takeaway
Japan regulates AI by soft law. The AI Promotion Act has no penalties and its enforcement tools are investigation, guidance and public naming. The first AI Basic Plan was adopted on 23 December 2025. Guidance for companies sits in the voluntary AI Guidelines for Business, now version 1.2 (31 March 2026). Financial-sector expectations come from the FSA's 2025 AI Discussion Paper.

### Cited Findings
- The AI Promotion Act passed the Diet on 28 May 2025, is principles-based and has no monetary penalties. [SS] — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/japans-first-ai-legislation-becomes-law-focus-promoting-research-and-development-no)
- It was promulgated on 4 June 2025. The chapters creating the AI Strategy Headquarters and the Basic Plan took effect in early September 2025 (1 September). [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/apac-ai-monitor-series-japan); [Nemko](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/ai-regulation-japan)
- Article 16 lets the government investigate and analyse risk cases and give guidance and advice to businesses. Enforcement is limited to guidance and naming. [SS] — [JD Supra](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ai-watch-global-regulatory-tracker-japan-1575214); [regulations.ai breakdown](https://regulations.ai/breakdowns/japan-ai-rd-act)
- The Cabinet adopted the first AI Basic Plan on 23 December 2025 [SS] — [Nippon.com/Jiji](https://www.nippon.com/en/news/yjj2025122300289). The official English provisional translation is titled "Japan Rebooted" through "Trustworthy AI" and is based on Article 18 [SS] — [Cabinet Office PDF](https://www8.cao.go.jp/cstp/ai/ai_plan/aiplan_eng_20260116.pdf)
- Version 1.2 of the AI Guidelines for Business was released by MIC and METI on 31 March 2026, after v1.0 (19 April 2024) and v1.1 (28 March 2025) [SS] — [timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/ai-business-guidelines-1-2-changes-2026). It remained the main business guidance in mid-2026 [SS] — [vorplabs](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/japan/2026-07/ai-promotion-act-basic-plan-business-guidance)
- Readings of v1.2 on agents conflict. One says "agentic AI" appears only in a footnote; another says v1.2 "significantly expanded coverage of AI agents" and made human-in-the-loop design explicit. [SS, conflicting] — [timewell](https://timewell.jp/en/columns/ai-business-guidelines-1-2-changes-2026)
- The FSA's AI Discussion Paper ("Preliminary Discussion Points for Promoting the Sound Utilization of AI in the Financial Sector") was published in Japanese on 4 March 2025 and in English on 10 April 2025. [SS] — [FSA](https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2025/20250304/aidp.html)
- According to Nishimura & Asahi, the paper says institutions "may employ human oversight or clearly disclose the use of generative AI" to address its risks, and stresses involvement by management. [SS] — [Nishimura & Asahi](https://www.nishimura.com/sites/default/files/newsletters/file/finance_law_robotics_artificial_intelligence_250818_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Japan sets no binding human-oversight, filing, labelling or local-representative duties for AI. Japanese entities' oversight duties come from the voluntary Guidelines and from sector supervision.

### Gaps
- I did not open the v1.2 text, so what it says on human oversight and agents is unverified.
- A reported revision of the Basic Plan on 14 July 2026 [SS] could not be confirmed.
- I did not research APPI cross-border transfer rules (Art. 28) this session.

## Singapore: Model AI Governance Framework, Agentic AI framework (Jan 2026), MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines (final Oct 2026), AI Verify and PDPA

### Takeaway
The Model AI Governance Framework for Agentic AI was launched on 22 January 2026 at Davos and updated on 20 May 2026. It is voluntary guidance. It makes humans "ultimately accountable" and asks organisations to define significant checkpoints where human approval is required. The MAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management were **finalised on 7 October 2026**. They cover all financial institutions and all AI, including agents, list human oversight among the lifecycle controls, and phase in from 7 October 2027 and 7 October 2028.

### Cited Findings
- The Agentic AI framework was launched on 22 January 2026 at the WEF in Davos by Minister Josephine Teo and developed by IMDA. It has four dimensions: (1) assess and bound risks upfront, "placing limits on agents' powers" including autonomy and access to tools and data; (2) human accountability: "Making humans meaningfully accountable for agents by defining significant checkpoints at which human approval is required"; (3) technical controls across the agent lifecycle, such as baseline testing and whitelisted services; (4) "Enabling end-user responsibility through transparency and education/training". [P] — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/singapore-launches-new-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai--/)
- The framework says "humans are ultimately accountable" and calls for "maintaining meaningful human control and oversight over agentic AI". It defines automation bias as "the tendency to over-trust an automated system that has performed reliably in the past". It calls itself guidance and "a living document". [P] — [MDDI](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/singapore-launches-new-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai--/)
- IMDA's factsheet on the updated framework is dated 20 May 2026 and was last updated on 21 May 2026. [P] — [IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/factsheets/2026/updated-model-ai-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai)
- The update added case studies. One describes a ticketing agent that by default needs human approval for editing files, running shell commands, making network requests or using external tools. The framework also recommends auditing how well human approvals work and adding automated monitoring. [SS] — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/supporting-the-safe-adoption-of-agentic-ai-singapore-launches-new-model-governance-framework-for-agentic-ai/)
- **MAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management** were issued on 7 October 2026 and "apply to all FIs and all forms of AI technologies". Boards and senior management provide "effective oversight of AI risks"; a dedicated AI committee is not required. FIs keep AI inventories "at an appropriate level of granularity" and assess the risk materiality of each use case. Lifecycle controls are "data governance, testing, human oversight, cybersecurity, monitoring and change management". FIs "remain accountable for AI used in the services they deliver", including third-party AI. [P] — [MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions)
- MAS timeline: the Guidelines take effect on 7 October 2027. Sections 3 and 4 apply from 7 October 2027 and Sections 5 and 6 by 7 October 2028. On agents, MAS notes "greater use of agentic AI systems that can operate autonomously and access tools" and will consult the sector in 2027 on further agentic guidance. [P] — [MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions)
- Per secondary sources, Sections 3–4 cover governance and core risk management and Sections 5–6 cover lifecycle controls, capability and infrastructure. The consultation opened in November 2025. [SS] — [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/34686/mas-publishes-ai-risk-management-guidelines-setting-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/10/singapore-mas-issues-guidelines-on-artificial-intelligence-risk-management)
- The PDPC's Advisory Guidelines on the Use of Personal Data in AI Recommendation and Decision Systems (1 March 2024) are non-binding. They clarify consent, notification and the exceptions to both across development, deployment and procurement, and do not cover generative AI. [SS] — [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/pdpc-issues-advisory-guidelines-on-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems/)

### Inferences
- Of the instruments in this set, the Singapore agentic framework is the most specific on human checkpoints for agents, and MAS's final guidelines are the first binding-in-effect supervisory text in this set that explicitly covers AI agents. That is the opposite of the US SR 26-2 exclusion recorded in trends.md.

### Gaps
- I did not open the framework PDF, so the exact checkpoint wording and the version number (a third party cites v1.5) are unverified.
- I did not research AI Verify or Project Moonshot status, the 2020 Model Framework or the 2024 GenAI framework, or the PDPA's Transfer Limitation Obligation this session.

## India: DPDP Act and Rules, AI Governance Guidelines, IT Rules on synthetic content, RBI FREE-AI

### Takeaway
India has no AI statute and the government says it is not regulating AI. The binding pieces are the IT Rules amendment on synthetically generated information (in force 20 February 2026: labelling, user declarations, fast takedown) and the DPDP regime. The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with consent managers live in November 2026 and the main obligations from about May 2027. The AI Governance Guidelines (November 2025) and RBI's FREE-AI (August 2025) are not binding.

### Cited Findings
- MeitY notified the DPDP Rules on 13 November 2025, with Gazette publication on 14 November. Definitions and the Data Protection Board apply at once. The consent-manager rules apply after 12 months (13 November 2026, though some say 12 November). The full rollout runs about 18 months, to 12 or 13 May 2027. [SS] — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708434/operationalising-indias-data-protection-regime-analysing-the-newly-notified-dpdp-rules-2025); [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025); [KSK](https://ksandk.com/md/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-consent-manager-framework-india/)
- Cross-border (Section 16 and Rule 15): transfers are allowed unless the Central Government restricts them, subject to conditions it may set. Under Rule 13(4), Significant Data Fiduciaries must localize categories of personal data that the government specifies on a committee's advice. One undated source says no SDF had been designated. Section 16 applies from about May 2027. [SS] — [KSK](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/indias-new-cross-border-data-transfer-framework/); [SFLC.in](https://sflc.in/?p=11194)
- The India AI Governance Guidelines were released by MeitY on 5 November 2025 under the IndiaAI Mission. They are voluntary and set out "Seven Sutras" (Trust; People First; Innovation over Restraint; Fairness & Equity; Accountability; Understandable by Design; Safety, Resilience & Sustainability). They rely on existing laws and sector regulators, not a new AI law. [SS] — [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines-to-ensure-safe-inclusive-and-responsible-ai-adoption); [India Infrastructure](https://indianinfrastructure.com/2025/11/07/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines-under-indiaai-mission/); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-unveils-indias-approach-towards-regulating-artificial-intelligence/)
- The IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Amendment Rules, 2026 were notified on 10 February 2026 and took effect on 20 February 2026. Synthetically generated information is audio, visual or audio-visual material made or altered by computer so that it appears real; whether text is covered is disputed. It must be labelled prominently. Significant social media intermediaries must obtain a user declaration before publication and use "reasonable and appropriate technical measures" for labelling and provenance. Hogan Lovells reports a 3-hour takedown for illegal content. [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/india-introduces-mandatory-labelling-for-ai-and-3hour-takedown-for-illegal-content); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/story/govt-tightens-rules-on-ai-generated-content-mandates-labels-and-faster-takedowns-515505-2026-02-10); [KSK](https://ksandk.com/information-technology/the-regulation-of-synthetic-media-in-india-legal-analysis/)
- RBI's FREE-AI committee report came out in August 2025. It sets 7 Sutras and 26 recommendations under 6 pillars, recommends a board-approved AI policy and governance structure for every regulated entity, and is not yet binding. [SS] — [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/13/dcm114-biz-rbi-ai.html); [law.asia](https://law.asia/rbi-ai-framework/); [FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/08/18/rbi-panel-calls-for-ai-framework-in-finance-sector/)
- Payment data localization (RBI circular of 6 April 2018; FAQs of 26 June 2019) is in force. Payment data must be stored only in India. If processed abroad, it must be deleted there and brought back within one business day or 24 hours, whichever is earlier. Sharing it with an overseas regulator needs RBI approval. [SS] — [RBI FAQ](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=130); [RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/citizens-corner/storage-of-payment-system-data-2995)

### Inferences
- An offshore oversight team can review Indian non-payment AI decisions today, subject to DPDP from 2027 and any negative list. Logs containing payment data must end up stored in India, and only a transient copy may sit abroad.

### Gaps
- I did not open the Gazette texts (DPDP Rules G.S.R. 846(E) and the IT Rules 2026), the MeitY guidelines PDF or the RBI FREE-AI report. FREE-AI's specific human-oversight recommendation was not found.
- Whether any SDF or negative-list country had been designated by 31 October 2026 is not confirmed.

## Australia: Guidance for AI Adoption, National AI Plan, Privacy Act ADM transparency and APRA

### Takeaway
Australia dropped the proposed mandatory guardrails for high-risk AI. The National AI Plan of 2 December 2025 relies on existing law and voluntary guidance, including the six-practice Guidance for AI Adoption (October 2025), one practice of which is "maintain human control". The binding items are a Privacy Act duty from **10 December 2026** to disclose automated decisions in privacy policies, and APRA's 30 April 2026 letter. That letter expects human involvement in high-risk decisions, controls over agentic workflows and continuous monitoring.

### Cited Findings
- The National AI Centre's Guidance for AI Adoption (October 2025; sources give 17 or 21 October) condenses the ten guardrails of the 2024 Voluntary AI Safety Standard into six practices: decide who is accountable; understand impacts and plan for them; measure and manage risks; share essential information; test and monitor; maintain human control. It is non-binding and comes in "Foundations" and "Implementation Practices" versions. [SS] — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/australia-launches-new-ai-guidance); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/australias-new-guidance-for-ai-adoption-a-strategic-step-toward-responsible-innovation); [industry.gov.au](https://www.industry.gov.au/publications/guidance-ai-adoption) (fetch timed out)
- The National AI Plan was released on 2 December 2025. The government will not mandate the proposed guardrails and will use existing technology-neutral laws plus voluntary guidance. The AI Safety Institute was announced on 25 November 2025 with just under A$30 million. Outlets differ on whether the guardrails were "paused" or abandoned. [SS] — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/australia-unveils-ai-policy-roadmap); [Gadens](https://www.gadens.com/insights/australia-launches-ai-safety-institute-and-releases-national-ai-plan); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/australia-introduces-a-national-ai-plan-four-things-leaders-need-to-know); [GovInfoSecurity](https://www.govinfosecurity.com/blogs/australia-abandons-proposed-mandatory-ai-rules-in-new-plan-p-3986)
- Privacy Act ADM transparency (APP 1, from the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024) is **Issued and applies from 10 December 2026**. From that date, APP entities must say in their privacy policy where "a computer program" makes or informs decisions that significantly affect individuals' rights or interests. Systems that materially assist a human decision-maker are covered. Policies must state the kinds of personal information used, and entities may group decisions by category. The OAIC published final guidance (APP 1 Guidelines, fact sheet, flowchart) in September 2026. [SS] — [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/new-resources-on-transparency-for-use-of-ai-and-automated-decision-making); [OAIC fact sheet](https://www.oaic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/269013/APP-1.7-1.9-Transparency-Obligation-Fact-Sheet.PDF); [Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/10/mandatory-automated-decision-making-disclosure-requirements-are-coming-key-takeaways-from-the-oaic-guidance/)
- APRA's Letter to Industry on AI (30 April 2026) goes to all APRA-regulated entities. It expects "human involvement for high-risk decisions and accountability", "controls over agentic and autonomous workflows", an inventory of AI tools and use cases, ownership "from design through decommissioning", mapping of AI supply chains including fourth parties, AI-specific change control, and assurance by continuous monitoring rather than point-in-time or sample reviews. It flags "the manipulation or misuse of autonomous AI agents" as a cyber threat. It says "existing prudential standards apply to AI risk" and that APRA may take stronger supervisory or enforcement action. [P] — [APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai)

### Inferences
- APRA's call for continuous monitoring over sample-based review is a primary-source counterpoint to trends.md §7.1, which says neither the US nor the Canadian guidance prescribes continuous review.

### Gaps
- I did not open the OAIC Guidelines text or the National AI Plan document itself.
- I did not research APP 8 cross-border disclosure. I know of no general localization rule, but did not verify that this session.

## Vietnam: Law on Artificial Intelligence (No. 134/2025/QH15)

### Takeaway
Vietnam has the most EU-like regime in the region, and it is now operational. The AI Law took effect on 1 March 2026. Decree 142/2026 (effective 1 May 2026) sets duties for high-risk AI providers and deployers, including human oversight, notification before deployment through a national portal, incident reporting within 72 hours and machine-readable labelling. A list of 46 high-risk systems (Decision 33/2026) took effect on 15 August 2026. Foreign providers of certain high-risk systems need a local presence or an authorised representative.

### Cited Findings
- The AI Law (No. 134/2025/QH15) took effect on 1 March 2026, with grace periods for systems already operating. Providers of other high-risk systems must at least keep a lawful local contact point. [SS] — [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/vietnam-ai-aw/); [Duane Morris](https://blogs.duanemorris.com/vietnam/2026/03/03/vietnam-the-first-law-on-artificial-intelligence-what-you-must-know/)
- Decree 142/2026/ND-CP was issued on 30 April 2026 and took effect on 1 May 2026. High-risk providers must run risk management systems that address "human oversight", and deployers must maintain "human oversight". Both must "preserve relevant logs and data" for serious incidents. Incident reports are due within 72 hours for urgent incidents or 5 working days for other serious ones, with follow-ups within 15 days. Before deployment, providers notify MOST of the risk classification "through the national AI one-stop portal", which issues an AI system identification code; the portal had not launched when the article was written. Third-party conformity certification applies only to certain high-risk categories; for the rest, self-assessment is allowed. AI audio, image and video need "machine-readable technical markings", and deployers must give notice where content could mislead. [S] — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-takes-major-step-in-ai-governance-with-new-guiding-decree/)
- Foreign providers of high-risk systems subject to mandatory certification must "establish a commercial presence or appoint an authorized representative in Vietnam". [S] — [Tilleke & Gibbins](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-takes-major-step-in-ai-governance-with-new-guiding-decree/)
- High-risk providers and deployers must be able to disable or temporarily halt the system and must meet lifecycle duties (risk management, data governance, documentation, human oversight, cooperation with regulators). Conformity assessment applies before deployment and after significant changes. [SS] — [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/vietnam-ai-aw/)
- Decision 33/2026/QD-TTg, the list of high-risk AI systems, was signed on 30 June 2026 and took effect on 15 August 2026. It names 46 systems in six groups: education, ethnicity and religion, healthcare, banking, litigation, and transport (the largest group). Many listed systems are high-risk only where the AI output is the sole basis for a decision without meaningful human review. Systems operating before 15 August 2026 get transitional periods. [SS] — [The Star/VNA](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/06/vietnam-releases-list-of-039high-risk039-ai-systems); [Tilleke](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-introduces-sectoral-list-of-high-risk-ai-systems/); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/33374/identifies-high-risk-ai-systems-across-six-sectors); [LuatVietnam text](https://english.luatvietnam.vn/decision-no-33-2026-qd-ttg-of-the-prime-minister-promulgating-the-list-of-high-risk-artificial-intelligence-systems-439294-doc1.html)
- Under the Personal Data Protection Law (Law 91/2025/QH15, in force 1 January 2026), cross-border transfers need a transfer impact assessment filed with the A05 police department, within 60 days of the first transfer according to one source. Baker McKenzie says it is "not an approval type of procedure" but can lead to restrictions. Fines for cross-border violations are reported as up to 5% of prior-year Vietnam revenue or VND 3 billion, but sources differ on how the cap works. Under the Law on Data, "critical" and "core" data need an assessment before transfer. [SS] — [Tilleke](https://tilleke.com/insights/vietnams-new-personal-data-protection-law-a-closer-look); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/?p=5530); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/31075/upcoming-changes-to-vietnam-s-personal-data-protection-regime)

### Inferences
- Vietnam's high-risk test depends on human review: a system can drop out of the high-risk category if a human meaningfully reviews its output, so documented human review becomes a classification lever as well as a control. This rests on one law firm's reading.

### Gaps
- I did not open the official text of the Law, the Decree or the Decision, so article numbers and penalty amounts for AI violations were not found.
- Whether the national AI portal has launched is unknown.
- Decree 53/2022 localization duties under the Cybersecurity Law were not researched this session.

## Taiwan: Artificial Intelligence Basic Act

### Takeaway
Taiwan's AI Basic Act (promulgated and effective 14 January 2026) is a 20-article framework law. It directs government, not companies. Companies face no direct obligations yet. MODA's AI Risk Classification Framework v1.0 (7 July 2026) tells sector regulators how to identify high-risk uses and expressly does not bind private AI applications.

### Cited Findings
- The Legislative Yuan passed the Act on 23 December 2025. It was promulgated and took effect on 14 January 2026, has 20 articles and names the National Science and Technology Council as central competent authority. [SS] — [Stanford FSI](https://fsi.stanford.edu/publication/taiwans-ai-basic-act-can-be-model-asia); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/taiwan-ai-basic-act)
- MODA released the "Artificial Intelligence Risk Classification Framework" (v1.0) on 7 July 2026 under Art. 16 of the Act. A scenario that could cause serious harm "should be classified as a high-risk AI application" (Art. 17(1)). The Framework "does not directly bind AI applications by non-government entities". Competent authorities follow four steps: inventory, identify (20 risk subtypes in 3 categories, including "post-deployment operations and human-machine interaction"), assess inherent risk, and respond proportionately where existing law has gaps. [S] — [Lee and Li](https://www.leeandli.com/EN/NewslettersDetail/7661.htm)
- The government must define responsibility and liability for high-risk AI, set up remedy, compensation or insurance mechanisms, and require clear warnings on high-risk products. [SS] — [IFLR](https://iflr.com/article/2gnkw0xfly6yrt21vokcg/sponsored/from-principles-to-practice-ai-governance-in-taiwan)
- Commentators disagree on whether the Act binds the private sector. Chambers says it imposes no private-sector obligations; others describe compliance requirements for enterprises. [SS, conflicting] — [Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/taiwan); [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/taiwan-ai-basic-act-explained)

### Inferences
- In Taiwan, oversight duties for a multinational will come from sector regulators (for example the FSC) using the MODA framework, not from the Basic Act.

## United Arab Emirates: federal posture, DIFC Regulation 10 and ADGM

### Takeaway
The UAE has no comprehensive federal AI statute. The strongest binding AI-oversight rule is DIFC Data Protection Regulation 10 on autonomous and semi-autonomous systems. It requires notices, human-defined purposes, algorithms that trigger human intervention, a use-case register, and an Autonomous Systems Officer for high-risk processing; secondary sources say full enforcement began on 1 January 2026. Amendments were consulted on in June and July 2026 and are not confirmed as enacted. The federal PDPL's executive regulations reportedly remain unissued.

### Cited Findings
- DIFC Regulation 10 dates from late 2023. A "Deployer" (deemed controller) or "Operator" (deemed processor) of a System must give a notice at first use. The notice discloses processing "not initiated or directed by humans" and whether the system can set its own purposes; human-defined purposes "must prevail". Systems need algorithms that trigger **human intervention** for potentially unfair or discriminatory outcomes, for lawful access by authorities, and for possible breaches of the digital-communication rules, each backed by risk and impact assessments. A register of system use cases records automated decisions, recipients and their locations, and export safeguards. No one may use a System for High Risk Processing unless the Commissioner has set certification requirements, or it serves only human-defined or human-approved purposes, or an **Autonomous Systems Officer** (with DPO-like status) has been appointed. [S] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/01/ai-regulation-in-the-difc-personal-data-processed-through-autonomous-and-semi-autonomous-systems)
- Full enforcement of Regulation 10 began on 1 January 2026 after a transition of more than two years. [SS, low-reliability source] — [noqta.tn](https://noqta.tn/en/news/difc-regulation-10-ai-compliance-dubai-2026)
- Draft: DIFC Consultation Paper No. 3 of 2026 (18 June to 18 July 2026) proposes refining Regulation 10 (safety-by-design and the ASO in Reg. 10.3.3) and adding Regulation 11 on accreditation and certification of autonomous systems. It is not confirmed as enacted. ## Saudi Arabia: SDAIA principles, PDPL and AI law drafts

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia has no AI statute. SDAIA's AI ethics principles and guidance are soft law. A draft Responsible AI Policy was open for consultation from 3 April to 3 May 2026 and has not been confirmed as final. The 2025 draft "Global AI Hub Law" concerns data embassies, not AI conduct. The binding constraint is the PDPL, fully enforceable since September 2024, with transfer rules amended in 2024 and Saudi SCCs.

### Cited Findings
- SDAIA's draft Responsible AI Policy was open for consultation from 3 April to 3 May 2026. It would apply to government bodies, companies, non-profits and individuals building or publishing AI in the Kingdom, with SDAIA as overseer. It is not confirmed as finalised. [SS] — [DPA](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/39086-saudi-data-and-artificial-intelligence-authority-opened-consultation-on-draft-responsible-ai-policy-including-data-protection-regulation)
- No dedicated AI law has been enacted. SDAIA's ethics principles, generative AI guidelines and adoption framework are soft law without fine schedules. [SS] — [Nemko](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/ai-regulation-in-saudi-arabia); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/ai-regulation-scanner/kingdom-of-saudi-arabia)
- The draft Global AI Hub Law was issued by the CST on 14 April 2025, with consultation to 14 May 2025. It sets up private, extended and virtual hubs (data embassies) and "did not propose an AI regulatory framework". No evidence was found that it has been finalised. [SS] — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/ksa-public-consultation-on-draft-ai-hub-law); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sau/legal-updates/shaping-the-future-of-data-sovereignty-saudi-arabia-issues-new-draft-global-ai-hub-law)
- PDPL transfers: amended Transfer Regulations were published on 1 September 2024 (some say August). The safeguards are SCCs (four versions, and any modification invalidates them), BCRs and certification. A transfer risk-assessment guideline followed in February 2025. As of November 2025, no adequacy list had been published. [SS] — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses); [Clyde & Co](https://clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/02/10-legal-updates-ksa-tech-and-data-laws); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/Insights/Articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law)

Sector rules (NCA cloud controls, NDMO, SAMA) reportedly restrict government and financial data from leaving. [SS, vendor sources, low reliability] — [Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/regulatory-compliance/saudi-banks-pdpl-compliance-controls/); [Clyde & Co](https://clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/02/10-legal-updates-ksa-tech-and-data-laws)

- In Saudi Arabia, AI oversight evidence about Saudi individuals may be reviewed abroad under SCCs or BCRs. For government or SAMA-regulated customers, assume the logs stay in the Kingdom unless the sector regulator has authorised otherwise.

## Data localization and cross-border transfer: where oversight evidence may be kept and who may see it

### Takeaway
Only China imposes a broad legal rule that keeps data in-country and blocks access from abroad, and it treats remote viewing as an export. India (payment data), Vietnam (critical and core data) and Saudi Arabia (government and some sector data) localize narrow categories. Korea, Japan, Singapore, Australia, Hong Kong, the UAE and Saudi Arabia (for general personal data) let a central team abroad review decisions once a transfer mechanism is in place. Several regimes also require records to be kept or produced: China's labelling logs for at least 6 months, Korea's draft 5-year records, Vietnam's incident logs, and the DIFC's use-case register.

### Cited Findings
- **China:** Art. 40 localization applies to CIIOs and threshold processors, and Art. 41 requires approval before data reaches foreign courts or enforcement bodies [SS] — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/part-3-pipls-localization-requirements-and-restrictions-on-responding-to-foreign-judicial-and-enforcement-agencies/). Remote access from abroad counts as an outbound transfer [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/key-questions-answered-chinas-regulation-of-international-data-transfers-effective-1-september-2022). Thresholds and routes are set by the 2024 Provisions and the 2026 Certification Measures [SS] — [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/china-finalizes-its-provisions-to-promote-and-regulate-cross-border-data-transfers); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/10/chinas-data-outbound-rules-update-measures-for-the-certification)
- **India:** DPDP transfers are open unless restricted (from about May 2027), with SDF localization for specified categories [SS] — [KSK](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/indias-new-cross-border-data-transfer-framework/). Payment data must be stored only in India; data processed abroad must return within 24 hours or one business day; sharing with an overseas regulator needs RBI approval [SS] — [RBI FAQ](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=130)
- **Vietnam:** a transfer impact assessment is filed with A05 under the PDPL, and critical or core data under the Law on Data need an assessment before transfer [SS] — [Tilleke](https://tilleke.com/insights/vietnams-new-personal-data-protection-law-a-closer-look); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/?p=5530)
- **Saudi Arabia:** SCCs, BCRs or certification are available; sector and government data are reportedly restricted [SS] — [Baker McKenzie](https://insightplus.bakermckenzie.com/bm/data-technology/saudi-arabia-updates-data-transfer-regulations-and-introduces-the-first-set-of-standard-contractual-clauses)
- **UAE:** federal PDPL Articles 22–23 apply, with executive regulations reportedly unissued [SS] — [Kayrouz](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/cross-border-data-transfers-under-uae-law-in-2026). The DIFC Reg. 10 register must list recipients "with their lawful bases and locations" and the export safeguards [S] — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2026/01/ai-regulation-in-the-difc-personal-data-processed-through-autonomous-and-semi-autonomous-systems)
- **Korea:** PIPA Art. 28-8 routes are consent, adequacy, SCCs and certification [SS] — [Legal 500 / Lee & Ko](https://legal500.com/firms/30803-lee-ko/c-south-korea/news-and-developments/major-amendment-to-the-personal-information-protection-act-passed-by-national-assembly-ii)
- **Records the regimes require:** China's labelling Art. 9 logs, kept at least 6 months [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm); China's GenAI Art. 14 duty to keep records of illegal user activity and report it [P] — [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm); Korea's 5-year default retention of oversight records (draft) [S] — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102low8/south-korean-ministry-of-science-and-ict-issues-package-of-regulations-to-supplem); Vietnam's preservation of logs for serious incidents [S] — [Tilleke](https://www.tilleke.com/insights/vietnam-takes-major-step-in-ai-governance-with-new-guiding-decree/); APRA's AI inventory and continuous monitoring [P] — [APRA](https://apra.gov.au/apra-letter-to-industry-on-artificial-intelligence-ai); MAS AI inventories [P] — [MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2026/mas-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-on-responsible-ai-adoption-by-financial-institutions)

### Inferences
- For a ~10-country storyboard, the defensible pattern is evidence held in-region and verdicts sent centrally. Raw prompts, outputs and decision logs about Chinese users (and Indian payment data) stay in-country, reviewed by local staff or exported only under a transfer mechanism. The central team sees de-identified metrics, control verdicts and pointers to the evidence. A foreign regulator's or parent's request for China-stored evidence needs Chinese approval under Art. 41. This is my synthesis, not a stated rule.
- "Who may see it" is mainly a China issue (remote access counts as export, plus the blocking rule) and an India payments issue (RBI approval for overseas regulators). Elsewhere it is a contractual and transfer-mechanism question.

### Gaps
- I did not verify this session Japan's APPI Art. 28, Singapore's PDPA s.26, Australia's APP 8, Hong Kong's PDPO s.33 status, Taiwan's PDPA, Vietnam's Decree 53 localization, or ADGM.
- I found no rule in any of these countries that explicitly addresses where *AI oversight logs* (as distinct from personal data) must be stored. Logs fall under these rules only to the extent they contain personal, payment, critical or important data.

## What in trends.md is now out of date or incomplete

### Takeaway
The four short APAC entries in trends.md (§4.2 Korea, §4.3 China, §4.4 Japan) need updating, one of its "Do not claim" lines on China is partly wrong, and it has nothing on eight of the eleven jurisdictions. The biggest additions for the talk: China's agent and human-like AI rules (2026), the final MAS guidelines covering AI agents (7 October 2026), the HKMA's planned registry of material AI agents (15 October 2026), APRA's continuous-monitoring expectation, and Vietnam's operational high-risk regime.

### Cited Findings
- **§4.2 Korea:** "Whether the enforcement decree has formally fixed the grace period: could not verify." The Act and Decree have both been in force since 22 January 2026. [S] — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/south-korea-ai-basic-act)

- These additions give the keynote's "Clock 1" an APAC counterpart.
