Compare — Akka vs. CrewAI

CrewAI is a framework. Akka is a platform.

CrewAI gets you to a multi-agent demo fast, and AMP can deploy it. With a framework you assemble the parts and own the result — availability, crash recovery, and the pager are yours. With a platform the system comes assembled and operated, under a contractual SLA.

CrewAI vs. Akka

A box of parts, or a running system.

CrewAI — Framework

You build it and own its uptime

CrewAI is a Python framework for building multi-agent systems — Crews of role-based agents and event-driven Flows. Crews and Flows are authoring components. Your engineers wire in crash recovery, high availability, and streaming around them. The Enterprise control plane, AMP, adds a visual editor, deployment, RBAC, SSO, tracing, task guardrails, and org-wide runtime policies on top, but the framework underneath is still an agent-authoring layer.

AMP deploys to CrewAI cloud, your VPC, or your own infrastructure. Resuming a crashed run is an explicit call in your code. There is no published application availability SLA, so the uptime of the running system is yours to guarantee.

Akka — Platform

It comes built and operated

Akka gives you the agents, durable state, streaming, APIs, and governance as one pre-integrated runtime. You describe the system you want, and the platform generates it, tests it, and runs it. Because everything runs in one runtime, a system that compiles is ready for production.

Akka keeps it running around the clock, with active-active replication and automatic failover, and stands behind a contractual 99.9999% availability SLA on the running system.

Akka Specify builds agentic systems from your specifications and releases only what meets your definition of done.

CrewAI — AMP policies

Inside CrewAI AMP

Guardrails, policies & approvals

With CrewAI, developers code task guardrails into each crew, and the @human_feedback decorator pauses a flow for human review. The Agent Control Plane applies 13 built-in policies across AMP deployments, enforcing PII masking, model and tool restrictions, and execution windows, and can pause and resume runs.

The Agent Control Plane governs only automations running on AMP. CrewAI publishes no mapping of its controls to the EU AI Act or any other regulation, and offers no pre-deployment classification or sealed audit artifact. AMP logs each human review request, response, and decision with a timestamp.

Akka — Enforce

Before the action

Block & escalate

Risk teams govern every agentic system without waiting on its developers, and every control they apply is guaranteed to be enforced. Risk teams choose from 1,718 controls drawn from 241 regulations and frameworks. Human-in-the-loop approvals, human-on-the-loop halt switches, sanitizers, guardrails, evaluations, testing gates, and simulations all run before an action executes and block or escalate anything that does not pass.

Every call is traced automatically into petabyte-scale, tamper-evident storage, and that same trace data feeds continuous evaluations that keep running in production, right alongside your agents.

Choosing between them

Pick for where you are.

Choose the framework when

Reach for the framework when you are prototyping or standing up a multi-agent system quickly, when the system is internal or low-stakes enough that some downtime is fine, and when your team is ready to own the crash recovery, high availability, and streaming that come with running it yourself. Authoring by hand is also the right call when you want low-level control over each crew and flow.

Choose the platform when

Reach for the platform when the system has to stay up, survive failure, and recover on its own, and when it has to pass an audit and enforce governance while it runs. Akka fits when you want a single vendor accountable for availability under an SLA, and when you would rather ship the system than build and operate the plumbing underneath it.
